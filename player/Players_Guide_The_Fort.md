# Player's Guide to The Fort

## *Everything you know. Everything everyone knows. Read this before we play.*

---

## The World

There was an empire. It fell. Nobody agrees on when or why.

The old roads are broken. The old cities are ruins. The old magic — and the old machines — are in the walls and in the ground, and sometimes they wake up. What's left are islands and coastlines and the settlements that cling to them. The seas between are dark and deep and things move in them. Trade ships come when they come. Soldiers don't come at all anymore.

The frontier is on its own. You are on the frontier.

---

## The Fort

That's what everyone calls it. A wooden palisade around thirty buildings at the edge of a lake, backed by forest, two days' walk from the nearest fishing village. It wasn't supposed to be permanent. It is now. About fifty people live here — twelve families, a militia, a merchant who comes and goes, and you.

### Places

| Location | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| **The Gate** | Faces the lake path. Heavy timber. One guard, most of the time. The palisade is sharpened logs, ten feet high. |
| **The Hall** | Largest building. Council fire, shared meals, a place to sleep if you don't have a roof. Captain Maren runs things from here. |
| **The Shrine** | Stone building, center of The Fort. Houses the Sacred Terminal — a screen set into a stone plinth, still powered by something nobody understands. Displays the traditional hymns on loop. People come to watch the words and leave offerings at the base. The Minister tends it. |
| **The Forge** | Anvil, bellows, coal. Makes tools and mends things. Can't make anything fancy. Cold right now. |
| **The Market** | Three stalls. Open when there's anything to sell. Old Harrow comes through every two or three weeks from the coast. |
| **The Beast Pen** | Behind the forge. Goats, chickens. One iron cage holding something the militia caught last month. |
| **The Docks** | Two rowboats, one small sailboat. The lake is calm most days. |
| **The Watchtower** | Palisade corner. You can see the cave ridge, the lake island, the forest canopy stretching south. |

The Terminal used to do more than loop hymns. It used to answer questions. It doesn't anymore — or at least, not reliably. People don't talk about that part.

### People

| Person | Role |
|--------|------|
| **Captain Maren** | Militia leader. Runs The Fort. Former imperial soldier. Practical, tired, loyal to this place. |
| **The Minister** | Tends the Terminal. Heals the sick. Reads the hymns over the dead. Whether she has guidance to give is between her and the screen. |
| **Old Harrow** | Traveling merchant from the fishing village. Tools, cloth, dried fish, lamp oil. Trades in gossip as much as goods. |
| **The Militia** | Six volunteers. Not soldiers. Spears, leather armor, one crossbow between them. |
| **The Families** | Farmers, woodcutters, a fisherman, a tanner. About fifty people total. Some have children. |

## What's Out There

You know this much. Everyone does.

| Direction | What's There |
|-----------|-------------|
| **South and West: The Forest** | Dense old-growth. Game trails, snare lines in the first half-mile. Old stone foundations deeper in. Wolves in winter, territorial boar, rumors of giant spiders. |
| **North: The Lake** | Big, deep, cold. Good fishing near shore. An island with old stone on it that nobody visits. Something comes close to shore at night. |
| **East: The Coast Path** | A day to the coast, two to the fishing village. Signs of strangers — bootprints, cold campfires. Bandits or scouts. |
| **Northwest: The Ridge** | Limestone ridge above the canopy. Cave mouths visible from the watchtower. Hunters avoid them. Animals avoid them. |

---

## The Current Situation

Last month, Captain Maren sent a three-person patrol to investigate strange lights near the ridge caves. Two came back confused and sluggish. The third — a militia volunteer named **Petra** — didn't return.

Three days ago, a woodcutter found Petra at the forest edge. Standing upright. Eyes open. Completely turned to stone.

Maren is looking for volunteers to go to the caves, find out what did this, and decide whether The Fort is in danger.

That's where you come in.

---

## What You Don't Know

A lot. The world is bigger than The Fort, older than the empire, and stranger than anyone here wants to admit. You'll learn by going out there and looking.

Your playbook tells you what *you* specifically know that others might not. Those details are yours — share them when it matters, or keep them close.

---

## How This Works at the Table

| Activity | What You Do |
|----------|-------------|
| **Explore** | Tell the DM where you go, what you look at, what you touch. The DM tells you what you find. |
| **Talk** | To each other, to NPCs, to the DM. When something matters to your character, say so. When you disagree, say that too. |
| **Fight** | Sometimes. Combat is dangerous and fast. You can die. Use your head before your sword. |
| **Declare** | Your Fate ability lets you add true facts to the world. You're not asking permission — you're telling everyone something that's real. The DM builds on it. |
| **Decide** | Every choice about what to do with what you find — keep it, sell it, destroy it, use it — is a choice about what kind of place this is going to be. |

> **Sidebar:** The question the game keeps asking: *What are we willing to do to survive out here?* You answer it by playing. There's no right answer. There's just what you choose and what happens next.
