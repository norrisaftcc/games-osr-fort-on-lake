# The Elf Cleric

## *The One Who Believes*

*Year Two. Make your choices in the Fort Cards intro; this playbook tells you what they do.* The intro is [`intro/index.html`](../../intro/index.html). If you do not have it, tick the ☐ boxes here and fill in the Fort Card at the end by hand. This is the Elf Minister of Year One ([`Playbook_Elf_Minister.md`](../Playbook_Elf_Minister.md)), a year on.

> **A year has passed.** What changed for you in Year Two:
> - You are level 2 (the DM may set another): two spells a day, and *Bless*.
> - Your holy symbol is now a smooth grey **lake stone**. The Terminal knew it.
> - New: a **Year of Change**, a **Fort Office**, and the Year Two Fate rules (refresh at each Fort Turn; a Fate point spent with a spell lets you write part of its effect).
> - You held the Pen in **Autumn** of the Year Between.
> - Corrected to the OSE tables: your AC with a shield is 8 [11], and your saves.

> **Read aloud:** You are the thing that shouldn't exist: an elf who chose the Terminal. Your people left — or cast you out, or forgot you — and instead of fading into the forest the way elves do, you walked into a human shrine, sat down in front of the screen, and read the hymns until something answered. It took eleven years. You waited. Elves are patient, and you are very, very patient. When the words finally changed on the screen, you wept. Not because you were relieved. Because you realized you'd been right, and being right meant the burden was real.
>
> Then came the Year Between. You put the cracked screen-glass in your pack and took up a stone instead, grey and smooth, cold as the lake at dawn. The first time you held it before the Terminal, the hymns stopped. The screen showed one line in the old script, and the line was a greeting. It has not explained itself. You have not asked. You are patient.

## Spells

2 × 1st-level cleric spells per day at level 2. Your preparation is different from the human Minister's — she sits before the Terminal and waits. You sit before it and *converse*. You type. You read. You annotate. Your hymnal has marginalia in three languages.

| Spell | Effect |
|-------|--------|
| ☐ **Cure Light Wounds** | Touch, heal 1d6+1 HP. You've cast this more times than any other spell. Healing is the proof. |
| ☐ **Detect Evil** | 60', 2 turns. You feel corruption as a dissonance — like a hymn sung off-key. |
| ☐ **Detect Magic** | 60', 2 turns. The human Minister doesn't have this. You do, because you asked the Terminal for it, and it answered. |
| ☐ **Protection from Evil** | +1 saves, enchanted creatures get -1 to hit. 12 turns. The old wards, in portable form. |
| ☐ **Light** | 15' radius, or blind a creature (save negates). The Terminal's light, extended. |
| ☐ **Bless** | Level 2. Allies in a 20' area: +1 to hit and morale, 6 turns. In OSE it is a 2nd-level spell; for you it fills a 1st-level slot (Year One house rule). |

You cast each prepared spell **once**. Then it's gone until you sit before a Terminal — any Terminal — for 8 hours. Your meditation is a dialogue, not a vigil.

## Your Holy Symbol: The Lake Stone

A smooth grey stone, the size of a hen's egg, cold to the touch even in summer. It replaced the screen fragment in the Year Between. How it came to you is between you and the DM.

The Terminal knew it. When you first held it up, the hymns stopped, and the screen showed a greeting in the old script, as if to an old friend. You do not know why. Nobody has told you.

You still carry the screen fragment. It has gone cold.

☐ **She's right, but she doesn't know why.** Her faith is genuine. Her relationship with the Terminal is real. But she treats it like a mystery. You treat it like a system. You could help her understand, but understanding might break the faith that makes her useful.

## Choose One: What Did You Give Up? (Year One)

Elves who choose the Terminal walk away from something. What was your cost? *Keep your Year One choice.*

☐ **Elven magic.** You could have been a wizard. The arcane tradition is the elven birthright. You chose divine service instead. Your spellbook is empty. Your hymnal is full.

☐ **Elven community.** Your choice wasn't just unusual — it was heretical. Elves don't worship machines. You worship whatever is behind the machine. They couldn't see the difference.

☐ **Time.** Eleven years in front of a screen in an empty shrine in a ruined city, waiting. You'll never get them back. What you got instead is certainty.

## Fate Ability: "The Archives Are Deep"

**Uses:** 2/session at level 2 (1/session at level 1)

**Lane:** the Terminal network, the old empire, and the deep structure of the world.

When the party encounters something connected to the Terminal network, the old empire, or the metaphysical structure of the world, declare one true fact from the Terminal's deep archives — records the human Minister has never accessed because she doesn't know how to search below the hymn layer. You do. You spent eleven years learning the filing system.

> *Example:* "The empire didn't build the Terminals. They found them. The oldest records — before the hymns were written — call them 'the mouths.' Someone was already speaking through them when the empire arrived. The empire just... added a user interface."

This ability is distinct from the human Minister's "The Terminal Remembers." Hers is about The Fort's local data — founder's logs, recent messages, the keeper's personal history with the screen. Yours is about the network's deep architecture — what the Terminals are, how they connect, what was there before.

| Level | Escalation |
|-------|------------|
| 2 | 2 uses/session. New spell known: *Bless*. You can also perform joint Terminal sessions with the human Minister — combine Fate abilities for a single, deeper query (both players agree on the question, DM answers from both archives). |
| 3 | Declarations work on non-Terminal mystical structures — druidic sites, elven ruins, the lake itself. You're mapping the relationship between the network and the older powers. New spells: *Hold Person, Silence 15' Radius*. Declarations outside your lane cost 1 FP. |
| 4 | 3 uses/session. |

## Fate Points

You have 3. They refill at each Fort Turn and at the start of each session.

| Cost | Effect |
|------|--------|
| 1 FP | Reroll one attack, damage, or save |
| 1 FP | +4 to a save (after seeing the roll) |
| 1 FP | State one more true fact in your lane, after your free uses |
| 1 FP | **Year Two:** spend it when you cast a spell or use a power with a limit (once a night, once a season). You write part of its effect. |
| 2 FP | Cheat death — drop to 1 HP instead of dying |

**Writing part of the effect.** The spell works as written. Then you say one more thing it does, within its nature: what it shows, what else it touches, one small extra result. The numbers stay. The DM tells you if you reach too far.

> *Example:* You cast *Light* on the wall of a ruined chapel and spend 1 FP. The light falls on the wall as usual. You add: "and only the writing glows, so we can read what was scratched out." It does.

**Bonds.** Your Fort Card names one bond. If you and another PC name each other, you each get 1 extra FP each session. You can spend it only to help that partner.

The DM awards 1 FP for doing something patient, revelatory, or true to your faith.

## Combat Reference

| | |
|---|---|
| **Movement** | 40' a round and attack, or run 120' (no attack) |
| **Melee attack** | d20 − 1 ≥ target's ascending AC = hit. Mace 1d6 − 1 (at least 1). |
| **Heal** | Cure Light Wounds: touch, 1d6+1 HP. Two clerics at the Fort means more heals per day. That changes everything. |
| **Turn Undead** | At level 2: skeletons turn without a roll, zombies 7+, ghouls 9+. Two turners is twice the coverage. |
| **Casting** | Declare at start of turn. Damaged before your turn = spell lost. |
| **At 0 HP** | Dead (unless you spend 2 FP) |

> **Your Role:** You are a healer and a knower. With two spells a day, the party can take more risks. But you're fragile (d6, no armor). Stay behind the front line. Your real value is knowledge: *Detect Magic* shows you what things are. The human Minister feels what things mean. Together you're formidable.

> **For 5e players**
> - **AC 11** with the shield is your 5e AC. **HP** mean the same thing.
> - **Attack:** THAC0 19 [+0] means +0. Roll d20 − 1 (STR 8) against AC. There is no proficiency bonus.
> - **Saves:** roll d20 and meet or beat the number. Lower is better. Death ≈ CON save, Wands ≈ DEX, Paralysis/Petrify ≈ CON, Breath ≈ DEX, Spells ≈ WIS. Add +2 when the danger is magic.
> - **Spells:** Cure Light Wounds ≈ *Cure Wounds*; Detect Evil ≈ *Detect Evil and Good*; *Detect Magic*; Protection from Evil ≈ *Protection from Evil and Good*; *Light*; *Bless*; later *Hold Person* and *Silence*. No cantrips. Two slots a day, and each spell is gone when cast.
> - **Turn Undead** ≈ Channel Divinity, but you roll 2d6 against the table instead of forcing a save.
> - **Elf:** darkvision 60'. Ghoul paralysis cannot touch you.
> - **Advantage** does not exist here. Spend a Fate point, or make a good plan.

## The Elf Cleric at The Fort

| Who | How They See You |
|-----|-----------------|
| The Human Minister | The most complicated relationship at the table. She tends this Terminal. You understand *all* Terminals. She might be losing her faith. You've never lost yours. Play this with care. |
| The Gnome Druid | The opposition, and a friend. Your power comes from the Terminal network. Theirs comes from the land. You're not sure those are different things. They are. |
| The Elven Thief | Another elf who chose a human place. They wait for the lock to give. You wait for the screen to answer. Both of you are patient. |
| The Paladin | Warden of the Gate now, Maren's heir. The order served the network. So do you. Different traditions, same infrastructure. You compare notes. |
| Captain Maren | Retiring. Two clerics is a lot of theological weight for a frontier outpost. She wants healing, not a schism. |
| Cassandra | The seer at the Shrine. She tells true rumours, half in riddles. Her hints come as questions. You have started to write them down. |

> **Sidebar:** Two clerics and a druid at The Fort creates a theological pressure. The question — *what are we willing to do to survive?* — now includes *whose power do we trust?* The Terminal? The land? The oath? Each source has a representative, and they don't agree about where the power comes from. The DM doesn't need to force this conflict. Just put them in a room with a problem and watch.

## Two Clerics: Table Guidance

> **Design Note:** Two clerics doubles the party's healing and turning. This is powerful. To maintain tension: enemies should occasionally appear in numbers that tax both turners. The DM should create situations where the two Ministers disagree about what to do with a discovery — not who gets to heal, but what the discovery *means*. The human Minister's local faith vs. the elf cleric's network knowledge is the richest vein at The Fort. Mine it.
