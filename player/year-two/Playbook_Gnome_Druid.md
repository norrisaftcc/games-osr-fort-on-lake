# The Gnome Druid

## *The One Who Listens*

*Year Two. Make your choices in the Fort Cards intro; this playbook tells you what they do.* The intro is [`intro/index.html`](../../intro/index.html). If you do not have it, tick the ☐ boxes here and fill in the Fort Card at the end by hand. This is the Gnome Druid of Year One ([`Playbook_Gnome_Druid.md`](../Playbook_Gnome_Druid.md)), a year on.

> **A year has passed.** What changed for you in Year Two:
> - You are level 2 (the DM may set another): two spells a day, and *Warp Wood* or *Heat Metal*. Your gnome illusion is still once a day.
> - New: a **Year of Change** and a **Fort Office**.
> - New Fate rules: refresh at each Fort Turn; a Fate point spent with a spell or your illusion lets you write part of its effect.
> - You held the Pen in **Spring** of the Year Between.
> - Corrected to the OSE tables: your saves.

> **Read aloud:** Nobody heard you coming. That's how it always is with gnomes — you're there, and then people realize you were there the whole time. You walked out of the deep forest barefoot, carrying a staff taller than you and a satchel full of things that smell like wet earth. You asked to see the shrine. The Minister showed you the Terminal. You looked at it for a long time, then said "that's not where the power comes from" and walked outside and sat under a tree. You've been there since, except when you wander off to talk to things that don't have mouths.
>
> Then came the Year Between, and you wrote its Spring. The thaw came early that year. The burrowing things have started to bring you news. Lately it is all the same news. The milestone on the Old Road hums, and the moles do not like it.

## Spells

2 × 1st-level druid spells per day at level 2. You don't memorize from a book or a screen. You sit on the ground, close your eyes, and listen. The land tells you what it needs you to do today.

| Spell | Effect |
|-------|--------|
| ☐ **Entangle** | Plants in a 20' area grab and hold creatures. Save or immobilized for 1 turn. The forest helps you. |
| ☐ **Faerie Fire** | Outline creatures in pale light — no invisibility, +2 to hit them, 20' radius. The land shows you what's hiding. |
| ☐ **Speak with Animals** | Talk to animals for 2 turns. They're not smart, but they're honest. A fox knows what it's seen. |
| ☐ **Purify Food and Water** | Enough for 12 people. You're already the most useful person at The Fort. The Minister knows this. |
| ☐ **Predict Weather** | Know the weather for the next 12 hours with certainty. On the frontier, this saves lives. |
| ☐ **Warp Wood** *or* ☐ **Heat Metal** | Level 2: choose one, for good. Both have fortress-defense uses. In OSE they are 2nd-level spells; for you the one you chose fills a 1st-level slot (Year One house rule). |

You cast each prepared spell **once**. Dawn meditation to prepare the next ones. No Terminal required.

## Choose One: What Does the Land Tell You? (Year One)

You listen to something the Minister can't hear. That's not a criticism — it's a fact. Her power comes from the screen. Yours comes from the dirt. The question is what the dirt is saying. *Keep your Year One choice.*

☐ **The land is sick.** Something is poisoning it — slowly, from underground, spreading outward from the ridge. The trees know. The burrowing animals are leaving. The water tastes wrong if you know what right tastes like.

| | |
|---|---|
| *Reputation* | Warning. You arrived with bad news. Maren listens because she has to. The Minister wonders if you're undermining the Terminal's authority. |
| *You start knowing* | Where the sickness is worst. A specific area near the caves where nothing grows. And one symptom the humans haven't noticed yet. |

☐ **The land is waking up.** Not sick — alive. More alive than it should be. New growth in places that should be barren. Animals acting strangely, not fleeing but watching. Something old is stirring, and it's not hostile. It's curious.

| | |
|---|---|
| *Reputation* | Strange. You spend too much time talking to trees. The children follow you. The adults aren't sure you're safe. |
| *You start knowing* | A place where the waking is strongest — a grove, a spring, a ring of stones. You've been going there alone. Something met you. It didn't speak. It just looked. |

☐ **The land is old, and it remembers.** The empire built over something. The Terminal network runs on something. The wards in the caves draw from something. The land was here before all of it, and it's patient, and it's waiting for the machines to stop.

| | |
|---|---|
| *Reputation* | Oracle. People come to you for weather and crop advice. They leave unsettled. You told the truth; it just wasn't comfortable. |
| *You start knowing* | That the Terminal and the land are connected — not allies, not enemies, but entangled. What happens to one affects the other. The Minister doesn't know this yet. You're deciding whether to tell her. |

## Choose One: Why Did You Come to The Fort? (Year One)

Gnomes live in burrows, in hollows, in the green underside of the world. What brought you out? *Keep your Year One choice.*

☐ **The animals told me to.** Not in words. In movement — migration patterns, a fox that led you three days through forest, birds that wouldn't let you turn south. The land wanted you here.

☐ **My grove was destroyed.** Fire, logging, something worse. The place I tended is gone. I need a new one. The lake shore might work, but something has to change first.

☐ **I heard the Terminal from underground.** A hum. Deep. Through the rock. Gnomes hear things humans can't. I followed the sound. It led here. I don't think the Terminal knows I can hear it.

## Choose One: What Do You Think of the Terminal? (Year One)

The Minister asked you, in the end. *Keep your Year One choice, or change it: a year has passed.*

☐ **It's a tool.** Like a plow or a loom. Useful, maybe. Holy, no. The power comes from the land. The screen just redirects it. Unplugging it wouldn't stop the healing — it might improve it.

☐ **It's a wound.** Something was driven into the earth here and it's still drawing from it. The hymns aren't prayer — they're a siphon. The Terminal takes from the land and gives to the keeper and nobody asks the land if that's okay.

☐ **It's a conversation.** The Terminal and the land are talking to each other. Slowly, in a language neither side fully speaks. The hymns are the land's side. The screen is the empire's side. You can hear both. You don't understand either.

## Fate Ability: "The Land Speaks"

**Uses:** 2/session at level 2 (1/session at level 1)

**Lane:** natural places — forest, lakeshore, natural cave, island — and what the earth, water, roots and animals know.

When the party is in a natural environment — forest, lakeshore, cave (natural, not constructed), island — declare one true fact about what the land knows. Not what you've studied or read — what the earth, the water, the roots, the animals are telling you right now. The land is a witness. It saw what happened here.

> *Example:* "The water table shifts east of this ridge. There's a hollow underneath — a big one. And the roots don't grow into it. They grow *around* it. Something's down there that the trees don't want to touch."

| Level | Escalation |
|-------|------------|
| 2 | 2 uses/session. New spell known: *Warp Wood* or *Heat Metal* (choose one — both have fortress-defense implications). |
| 3 | Declarations work on constructed environments — you can feel where the building meets the earth, where the foundation is strong, where the land is rejecting what's been built on it. Declarations outside your lane cost 1 FP. |
| 4 | 3 uses/session. |

## Fate Points

You have 3. They refill at each Fort Turn and at the start of each session.

| Cost | Effect |
|------|--------|
| 1 FP | Reroll one attack, damage, or save |
| 1 FP | +4 to a save (after seeing the roll) |
| 1 FP | State one more true fact in your lane, after your free uses |
| 1 FP | **Year Two:** spend it when you cast a spell or use a power with a limit (your illusion, once a day). You write part of its effect. |
| 2 FP | Cheat death — drop to 1 HP instead of dying |

**Writing part of the effect.** The spell works as written. Then you say one more thing it does, within its nature: what it shows, what else it touches, one small extra result. The numbers stay. The DM tells you if you reach too far.

> *Example:* You use *Ghost Sound* and spend 1 FP. The sound comes from 60' away, as usual. You add: "and it is Captain Maren's voice, calling the militia to the gate." The bandits hear exactly that.

**Bonds.** Your Fort Card names one bond. If you and another PC name each other, you each get 1 extra FP each session. You can spend it only to help that partner.

The DM awards 1 FP for doing something true to the land, inconvenient for the community, or wise.

## Combat Reference

| | |
|---|---|
| **Movement** | 30' and attack (short legs), OR run 90' (no attack) |
| **Melee attack** | d20 − 1 ≥ target's ascending AC = hit. Staff 1d6 − 1 (at least 1). |
| **Missile attack** | d20 + 0 ≥ target's ascending AC. Sling 1d4. |
| **Casting** | Declare at start of turn. Damaged before your turn = spell lost. |
| **Gnome defense** | -2 to AC vs. creatures larger than human-sized (they can't hit what they can't reach) |
| **At 0 HP** | Dead (unless you spend 2 FP) |

> **Your Role:** Control and utility. *Entangle* shuts down a room the way *Sleep* does, but it needs plants — outdoors is your domain. *Faerie Fire* helps the whole party hit. *Speak with Animals* is intelligence gathering. You're not a healer (that's the cleric's job), but your herb pouch stabilizes the dying. Underground, look for growth: moss, roots, fungus. Where something grows, *Entangle* works.

> **For 5e players**
> - **AC 12** is your 5e AC (14 against anything bigger than a human). **HP** mean the same thing.
> - **Attack:** THAC0 19 [+0] means +0. Roll d20 − 1 (STR 8) in melee, d20 + 0 with the sling, against AC. There is no proficiency bonus.
> - **Saves:** roll d20 and meet or beat the number. Lower is better. Death ≈ CON save, Wands ≈ DEX, Paralysis/Petrify ≈ CON, Breath ≈ DEX, Spells ≈ WIS. Add +1 when the danger is magic.
> - **Spells:** *Entangle*; *Faerie Fire*; *Speak with Animals*; Purify Food and Water ≈ *Purify Food and Drink*; Predict Weather has no 5e spell (think *Druidcraft*, but certain); *Heat Metal*; Warp Wood has no 5e spell. Illusions: *Dancing Lights*; Ghost Sound ≈ *Minor Illusion* (sound only). No cantrips. Two slots a day, and each spell is gone when cast.
> - **Gnome:** darkvision 60'; speak with burrowing mammals ≈ the forest gnome's *Speak with Small Beasts*; in the wild you leave no trail, as *Pass without Trace* on yourself.
> - **Advantage** does not exist here. Spend a Fate point, or make a good plan.

## The Gnome at The Fort

You are the smallest person here and possibly the most dangerous to the community's assumptions.

| Who | How They See You |
|-----|-----------------|
| The Minister | Threatened. Not by you — by what you represent. If the power comes from the land and not the Terminal, what is she for? |
| The Elf Cleric | The opposition, and a friend. Their power comes from the network. Yours comes from the land. They aren't sure those are different things. You are. |
| The Elven Thief | Nobody hears either of you coming. You have argued about which of you is quieter. Nobody has won. |
| Captain Maren | Useful. You predict weather, purify food, talk to animals. She doesn't care about your theology. She retires soon. |
| The Paladin | Warden of the Gate now, Maren's heir. Your power doesn't come from the oath or the network. It just... is. That bothers someone who needs structure. |
| Wick and the children | Fascinated. You're their height. You talk to foxes. Wick counts the things you grow. |
| Biddy | Everyone keeps clear of her beak. Animals tell you what she thinks of that. She is pleased. |

> **Sidebar:** The druid's neutrality requirement creates a different kind of pressure. The paladin must act lawfully. The Minister must act faithfully. You must act *naturally* — and nature doesn't care about human survival. When the question is "what are we willing to do to survive," your answer might be "that depends on whether the land agrees."
