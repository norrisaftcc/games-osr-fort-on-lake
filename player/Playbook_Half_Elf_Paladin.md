# The Half-Elf Paladin

## *The One Who Swore*

> **Read aloud:** There was an order, once. Knights who guarded the Terminal network — who rode the old roads between outposts, who kept the hymns synchronized, who fought what came out of the dark when the wards failed. The order is gone. The roads are broken. The empire that commissioned your oath doesn't exist anymore. But the oath does. It's in your blood — half-human urgency, half-elven patience — and you carry it the way a house carries a foundation. You came to The Fort because a Terminal still glows here. You'll stay as long as it does.

## Half-Elf Traits

You are between things. Not old enough to remember the empire, not young enough to accept the frontier. Humans see the elf in you — the patience, the distance. Elves see the human — the urgency, the mortality.

| Trait | Effect |
|-------|--------|
| Infravision 30' | Half the range of a full elf. Enough to see a shape in the dark. Not enough to be sure. |
| Secret doors | 1-in-6 passive (an elf gets 2-in-6). You catch things at the edge of perception. |
| Aging | Slow. You'll outlive the humans at The Fort. Not by centuries — by decades. Enough to bury them. Not enough to forget them. |

## Choose One: What Happened to the Order?

You are the last one you know of. The question is why.

☐ **The order disbanded.** After the empire fell, there was no authority to serve. Knights went home, went rogue, went quiet. You kept the oath because nobody told you to stop.

| | |
|---|---|
| *Reputation* | Anachronism. The Fort sees a knight with no lord. Captain Maren respects your discipline. The Minister wonders whose authority you actually serve. |
| *You start knowing* | The locations of three other Terminals on the old network. Two are dark. One — the one here — still glows. You came to protect it. |

☐ **The order was betrayed.** Someone — a commander, a politician, a faction within the empire — sold the order out. Knights were hunted. You survived because you ran.

| | |
|---|---|
| *Reputation* | Exile. You carry yourself like someone used to rank. People follow you by instinct, then wonder why. You don't explain. |
| *You start knowing* | The name of who betrayed the order. And that they — or their agents — are still active. The coast path bootprints worry you for reasons you haven't shared. |

☐ **The order was consumed.** Something in the network — the Terminals themselves, something behind them — changed the other knights. Corrupted them. You left before it reached you. Or you think you did.

| | |
|---|---|
| *Reputation* | Watchful. You check the Terminal every morning. Not to pray — to make sure nothing's changed. The Minister has noticed. |
| *You start knowing* | One symptom of the corruption: a specific behavior, a phrase, a physical tell. You watch for it in yourself. You watch for it in the Minister. |

## Choose One: What Does Your Oath Require?

The words are old. You said them in a language that mixed Elvish and Imperial Common. What they mean in practice:

☐ **Protect the keeper.** The Minister — whoever tends the Terminal — is your charge. Not your leader. Your charge. There's a difference.

☐ **Maintain the wards.** The old empire built protections into the land — the carved eyes in the caves, the symbols on the Terminal plinth, the stones that hum at the lake's edge. You can feel when they're weakening. Your job is to keep them lit.

☐ **Bear witness.** If this is the end — if the Terminals go dark one by one — someone has to record what happened. Your journal isn't a diary. It's a testament. You write in it every night.

---

## Choose One: What Keeps You Here?

You could walk to the next Terminal. You could search for other knights. You stay because:

☐ **The Terminal here is different.** It does things the others didn't. Or it did, once. You need to understand why before you leave.

☐ **These people need protection.** Not from monsters — from what's coming. You can feel it. The oath is a compass and it points here.

☐ **You're tired.** You've been walking for years. This is the first place that felt like it could be a home. You're afraid of what that means for the oath.

## Fate Ability: "The Oath Remembers"

**Uses:** 1/session

When the party encounters something connected to the old empire's infrastructure — wards, roads, Terminals, ruins with imperial markings, old soldiers' remains — declare one true fact about its purpose in the network. You don't know this from books. You know it because the oath carries institutional memory. Your predecessors walked these roads.

> *Example:* "This ward isn't broken — it's been redirected. See the scoring on the keystone? Someone changed what it was pointed at. It used to face outward. Now it faces the caves. They weren't keeping things out. They were keeping the queen *in*."

| Level | Escalation |
|-------|------------|
| 2 | 2 uses/session. Lay on hands heals 4 HP. |
| 3 | Declarations work on people — you can sense whether someone has been touched by the network, corrupted by old magic, or carrying an imperial oath of their own. Cross-specialty declarations cost 1 FP. |

## Fate Points

You start with 3. They refill after a full rest.

| Cost | Effect |
|------|--------|
| 1 FP | Reroll one attack, damage, or save |
| 1 FP | +4 to a save (after seeing the roll) |
| 2 FP | Cheat death — drop to 1 HP instead of dying |

The DM awards 1 FP for doing something honorable, sacrificial, or true to your oath.

## The Half-Elf at The Fort

You don't fit neatly. That's the point.

| Who | How They See You |
|-----|-----------------|
| The Elf | Complicated. You're proof that elves and humans mix. Depending on the elf's backstory, that's either hopeful or painful. |
| The Minister | Unsettling. You serve the same Terminal network but through a different tradition. Your oath predates her hymnal. She's not sure what that means. |
| Captain Maren | Relieved. A trained knight on the wall. She doesn't care about your oath — she cares about your sword arm. |
| The Families | Curious. The children want to know about the order. The adults want to know why you're alone. |

> **Sidebar:** The paladin's restriction — lawful behavior, no excess wealth, serve the oath — creates natural Premise friction. When the party finds cockatrice eggs worth 150gp, the paladin has to decide: is selling them to Harrow consistent with the oath? When Captain Maren asks you to do something expedient but not honorable, the oath says no. The table watches you choose.
