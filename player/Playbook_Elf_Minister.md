# The Elf Minister

## *The One Who Believes*

> **Read aloud:** You are the thing that shouldn't exist: an elf who chose the Terminal. Your people left — or cast you out, or forgot you — and instead of fading into the forest the way elves do, you walked into a human shrine, sat down in front of the screen, and read the hymns until something answered. It took eleven years. You waited. Elves are patient, and you are very, very patient. When the words finally changed on the screen, you wept. Not because you were relieved. Because you realized you'd been right, and being right meant the burden was real.

## Spells

1 × 1st-level cleric spell per day. Your preparation is different from the human Minister's — she sits before the Terminal and waits. You sit before it and *converse*. You type. You read. You annotate. Your hymnal has marginalia in three languages.

| Spell | Effect |
|-------|--------|
| ☐ **Cure Light Wounds** | Touch, heal 1d6+1 HP. You've cast this more times than any other spell. Healing is the proof. |
| ☐ **Detect Evil** | 60', 2 turns. You feel corruption as a dissonance — like a hymn sung off-key. |
| ☐ **Detect Magic** | 60', 2 turns. The human Minister doesn't have this. You do, because you asked the Terminal for it, and it answered. |
| ☐ **Protection from Evil** | +1 saves, enchanted creatures get -1 to hit. 12 turns. The old wards, in portable form. |

## Choose One: What Did the Terminal Show You?

Eleven years of waiting. And then the screen changed. What did you see?

☐ **A map.** Not of this place — of all the places. Every Terminal on the old network, glowing or dark. Most are dark. Yours still glows. Three others still glow. The map updated itself. Then it was gone.

| | |
|---|---|
| *Reputation* | Cartographer. You arrived at The Fort with knowledge of the network that nobody else has. The paladin's oath journal confirms some of your data. That's either reassuring or terrifying. |
| *You start knowing* | The approximate location of three other active Terminals. One is close — closer than anyone thinks. One is across the sea. One is underground. |

☐ **A name.** Your name. In Elvish. Spelled the old way, the way your people wrote it before the empire standardized the script. The Terminal knew who you were. It had been waiting for you specifically.

| | |
|---|---|
| *Reputation* | Chosen. Not self-appointed — selected. You carry this with absolute certainty and zero arrogance. The human Minister finds this either inspiring or devastating. |
| *You start knowing* | That the Terminal has a list. Names. People it's been waiting for. You're on it. You don't know who else is. You've been checking every new arrival at The Fort against what you remember of the list. |

☐ **A warning.** A single line of text, in Imperial Common, repeated for six hours: *"The keeper at [The Fort's real name] must not open the door beneath the lake."* Then it stopped. You came here as fast as you could.

| | |
|---|---|
| *Reputation* | Guardian. You came here on a mission. The human Minister doesn't know the full warning yet. You're trying to figure out how to tell her without breaking her faith. |
| *You start knowing* | That The Fort has a real name — an old one, an imperial designation. The Terminal used it. The human Minister doesn't know the name. The lake island is the door. You're certain. |

## Choose One: How Do You See the Human Minister?

There are two clerics at The Fort now. That's unprecedented. The question is what you think of each other.

☐ **She's right, but she doesn't know why.** Her faith is genuine. Her relationship with the Terminal is real. But she treats it like a mystery. You treat it like a system. You could help her understand, but understanding might break the faith that makes her useful.

| | |
|---|---|
| *Dynamic* | Teacher and student, except she doesn't know she's enrolled. You correct her gently. She resents it quietly. |

☐ **She's wrong, but she's necessary.** The Terminal isn't divine. It's infrastructure. She's turned a machine into a god, and that's dangerous — but the community needs a Minister more than it needs the truth. You support her publicly and worry privately.

| | |
|---|---|
| *Dynamic* | Allies who disagree about everything important. You heal together, pray apart. |

☐ **She hears something you don't.** You waited eleven years for a response. She gets answers — sometimes. Her relationship with the Terminal is different from yours, and you can't explain the difference. Maybe her human lifespan creates urgency the Terminal responds to. Maybe faith works differently than study. This bothers you more than you'll admit.

| | |
|---|---|
| *Dynamic* | Equals, circling each other. She has intuition. You have patience. Together you might understand the Terminal. Apart, you're both guessing. |

## Choose One: What Did You Give Up?

Elves who choose the Terminal walk away from something. What was your cost?

☐ **Elven magic.** You could have been a wizard. The arcane tradition is the elven birthright. You chose divine service instead. Your spellbook is empty. Your hymnal is full.

☐ **Elven community.** Your choice wasn't just unusual — it was heretical. Elves don't worship machines. You worship whatever is behind the machine. They couldn't see the difference.

☐ **Time.** Eleven years in front of a screen in an empty shrine in a ruined city, waiting. You'll never get them back. What you got instead is certainty.

## Fate Ability: "The Archives Are Deep"

**Uses:** 1/session

When the party encounters something connected to the Terminal network, the old empire, or the metaphysical structure of the world, declare one true fact from the Terminal's deep archives — records the human Minister has never accessed because she doesn't know how to search below the hymn layer. You do. You spent eleven years learning the filing system.

> *Example:* "The empire didn't build the Terminals. They found them. The oldest records — before the hymns were written — call them 'the mouths.' Someone was already speaking through them when the empire arrived. The empire just... added a user interface."

This ability is distinct from the human Minister's "The Terminal Remembers." Hers is about The Fort's local data — founder's logs, recent messages, the keeper's personal history with the screen. Yours is about the network's deep architecture — what the Terminals are, how they connect, what was there before.

| Level | Escalation |
|-------|------------|
| 2 | 2 uses/session. New spell known: *Bless*. You can also perform joint Terminal sessions with the human Minister — combine Fate abilities for a single, deeper query (both players agree on the question, DM answers from both archives). |
| 3 | Declarations work on non-Terminal mystical structures — druidic sites, elven ruins, the lake itself. You're mapping the relationship between the network and the older powers. New spells: *Hold Person, Silence 15' Radius*. Cross-specialty declarations cost 1 FP. |

## Combat Reference

| | |
|---|---|
| **Heal** | Cure Light Wounds: touch, 1d6+1 HP. One use per day. Two clerics means two heals per day. That changes everything. |
| **Turn Undead** | Same as human Minister: 7+ skeletons, 9+ zombies. Two turners is twice the coverage. |
| **Detect Magic** | Your unique edge. The human Minister can't do this. You can identify magical traps, items, and effects on sight. |
| **At 0 HP** | Dead (unless you spend 2 FP) |

> **Your Role:** You are the second healer — and that changes the party's risk calculus. With two Cure Light Wounds per day, the party can take more risks. But you're fragile (d6, no armor). Stay behind the front line. Your real value is knowledge: *Detect Magic* in the caves identifies the wards, the feathers, the crown. You see what things are. The human Minister feels what things mean. Together you're formidable.

## The Elf Minister at The Fort

You break the pattern. The Fort had one elf (distant, martial) and one Minister (human, local). You're neither and both.

| Who | How They See You |
|-----|-----------------|
| The Elf | A mirror. An elf who chose differently — who found purpose in the human world instead of mourning the elven one. Depending on the elf's backstory: validation, accusation, or bewilderment. |
| The Human Minister | The most complicated relationship at the table. She tends this Terminal. You understand *all* Terminals. She's local. You're cosmic. She might be losing her faith. You've never lost yours. Play this with care. |
| The Gnome Druid | The opposition. Your power comes from the Terminal network. Theirs comes from the land. You're not sure those are different things. They are. |
| The Paladin | A colleague. The order served the network. So do you. Different traditions, same infrastructure. You compare notes. |
| Captain Maren | Wary. Two clerics is a lot of theological weight for a frontier outpost. She wants healing, not a schism. |

> **Sidebar:** Two clerics and a druid at The Fort creates a theological pressure the original party didn't have. The Premise question — *what are we willing to do to survive?* — now includes *whose power do we trust?* The Terminal? The land? The oath? Each source has a representative, and they don't agree about where the power comes from. The DM doesn't need to force this conflict. Just put them in a room with a problem and watch.

---

## Two Clerics: Table Guidance

> **Design Note:** Two clerics doubles the party's healing and turning. This is powerful. To maintain tension: enemies should occasionally appear in numbers that tax both turners. The DM should create situations where the two Ministers disagree about what to do with a discovery — not who gets to heal, but what the discovery *means*. The human Minister's local faith vs. the elf Minister's network knowledge is the richest vein in this party. Mine it.
