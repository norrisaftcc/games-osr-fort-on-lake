# The Elf

## *The One Who Stayed*

> **Read aloud:** There's one elf at The Fort. You. The others left — your people, your kin, whoever they were — a long time ago. Or maybe you're the one who left them. Either way, you're here now, among humans who live fast and die easy, and they look at you like you're a relic. A reminder that the world used to be different. You can fight and you can cast spells and you don't age the way they do, and none of that makes this easier.

## Spells

You cast arcane spells like a wizard AND fight like a fighter. 1 × 1st-level spell per day. Pick one:

| Spell | Effect |
|-------|--------|
| ☐ **Sleep** | 2d8 HD of creatures fall asleep. No save. The best spell at level 1. |
| ☐ **Charm Person** | One humanoid treats you as a trusted friend. Save negates. You've used this before to avoid killing. |
| ☐ **Magic Missile** | 1d6+1, auto-hit. Clean. No ambiguity. |
| ☐ **Light** | 15' radius on an object or creature's eyes (blinding, save negates). 12 turns. You don't need torches. The humans do. |

You cast your prepared spell **once**, then it's gone until you study your spellbook (8 hours). Yes, you have a spellbook. No, you don't talk about it.

## Choose One: Why Are You the Only One?

This is the question The Fort doesn't ask out loud. The children whisper it. The adults think it.

☐ **Your people are gone.** Not dead — gone. They left when the empire fell. Sailed west, walked into the deep forest, crossed to somewhere else. You were supposed to go with them.

| | |
|---|---|
| *Reputation* | Relic. The community treats you with cautious reverence. They ask about the old days as if you were there for all of them. (You weren't.) |
| *You start knowing* | Where your people went. Or at least, the direction. And one reason you've never followed. |

☐ **You were sent.** Your people are out there, somewhere. They sent you to this outpost — a watcher, a guardian, a test. You don't know which. The instructions were vague: "Go to the lake. Wait. You'll know when it's time." It's been two years.

| | |
|---|---|
| *Reputation* | Guardian. The community assumes you're protecting them. You let them think that. It's simpler than the truth. |
| *You start knowing* | A sign you were told to watch for. It hasn't appeared yet. Or maybe it has, and you missed it. |

☐ **You were cast out.** You did something your people don't forgive. You won't say what. The Fort took you in the way it takes in everyone — grudgingly, because the frontier needs bodies.

| | |
|---|---|
| *Reputation* | Exile. Some trust you more for it — you're not above them, you're with them. Others trust you less. An elf without a people is an elf without a leash. |
| *You start knowing* | What you did. And one person at The Fort who reminds you of someone you wronged. |

## Choose One: What Do They Ask You About?

The community comes to you for one thing, over and over, because you're the oldest person they've ever met.

☐ **The old empire.** "What was it like? Why did it fall? Could it come back?" You don't know the answers. But you know more than they do.

☐ **The monsters.** "What are those things? Where do they come from? Can they be killed?" You've seen most of them before. Some used to be different.

☐ **The dead.** "Where do people go? Is there something after?" They ask the Minister for faith. They ask you because you're old enough to have seen proof. You haven't. But you've seen things.

## Fate Ability: "I Remember When"

**Uses:** 1/session

When the party encounters something old — a ruin, an artifact, a creature, a place — declare one true fact about what it was before. You're not guessing. You were alive when some of these things were different. Or you know someone who was.

> *Example:* "This wasn't a cave. It was a mine. The dwarves cut these walls — you can see the chisel marks under the limestone. They abandoned it. But not because it was empty."

Your declarations carry the weight of a long life. They aren't bookish — they're personal. You remember.

| Level | Escalation |
|-------|------------|
| 2 | 2 uses/session |
| 3 | Declarations work on living people and cultures. You've watched human families for generations. You see patterns they can't. Cross-specialty declarations cost 1 FP. |

## Fate Points

You start with 3. They refill after a full rest.

| Cost | Effect |
|------|--------|
| 1 FP | Reroll one attack, damage, or save |
| 1 FP | +4 to a save (after seeing the roll) |
| 2 FP | Cheat death — drop to 1 HP instead of dying |

The DM awards 1 FP for doing something bold, patient, or true to your character.

## Combat Reference

| | |
|---|---|

| **Casting** | Declare at start of turn. Damaged before your turn = spell lost. |

> **Your Role:** You're the hybrid — sword and spell. Not as tough as the fighter (d6, not d8), not as versatile as the wizard (same spell count, but you can stab). You fill gaps. When the fighter goes down, you hold the line. When the wizard is spent, you still have options.

## The Only One

You are the only non-human at The Fort. This means:

| Who | How They See You |
|-----|-----------------|
| Children | Stare. Some fascinated. Some afraid. One has started following you around. |
| Adults | Too polite. They don't know how to talk to someone who might outlive their grandchildren. |
| The Minister | An equal or a question mark. You've been alive longer than their faith. That's either reassuring or threatening. |
| Captain Maren | Respects your abilities. Doesn't ask personal questions. You appreciate this. |

> **Sidebar:** When the Premise presses — *what are we willing to do to survive?* — your answer carries a different weight. You'll be here after they're gone. Whatever The Fort becomes, you'll live with it longest.
