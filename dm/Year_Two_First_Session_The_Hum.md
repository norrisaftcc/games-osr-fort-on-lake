# Year Two, First Session

## *The Hum*

*One session, two to three hours, for the three Year Two PCs at level 2: the elven thief, the elf cleric of the Order of the Sacred Terminal, and the gnome druid. GM-facing. Players never see this file.*

**Map:** [The Hum, DM copy](maps/year_two_the_hum.svg) (the four hexes travelled, and a 10-foot plan of the outflow tunnel's five stations).

## The Job

The milestone on the Old Road has hummed for as long as anyone remembers. A month ago the hum changed. Now it comes in pulses: three, then four, then five, then one long note, over and over. The same week, water began to run at midnight out of a stone mouth in the bank at the Weirs, a mouth that has been dry in living memory. The Quiet have built kingfisher shrines along the new stream. Three weeks ago an agent of the Bureau of Imperial Anomalies went to look. He nailed a map inside the hunter's lodge at Boar Hollow, marked it with a red X, and did not come back. The party goes to find where the water comes from and what the hum is calling for, before it stops calling.

> **Art reference:** Elmore paint. Dawn on the Old Road. Three figures at the milestone: a tall elf in a grey hood with one hand flat on the stone, an elf in a pale vestment holding up a smooth grey stone on a cord, and a gnome with a staff and a crow on the shoulder. The milestone is darker than the trees behind it. Far off, a line of reeds and a thread of water catching the light.

### The Clock

Two clocks run. Tell the players about neither. Let them find both.

**The long clock: the long notes.** Each midnight the hum ends with some long notes. Wick has counted them: seven, six, five, four. Tonight there will be three. When they reach none, the hum stops for good.

| Midnight | The hum | What it means |
|----------|---------|---------------|
| Tonight (1st) | 3 long notes | The station is still calling. The water runs for one hour. |
| 2nd | 2 long notes | The same. The Junior Men in Red come to the Weirs and wait at the mouth. |
| 3rd | 1 long note | The same. The Quiet sit at their shrines all night. |
| 4th | None | **The station gives up.** The hum stops. The water stops. The inner door locks. If Agent Platt is still in T4, his water stops too. |

If the party is slow, nobody answers the call, the stream dries, and the Bureau files the hum as "self-resolved". That is a fair result. Say so when it happens.

**The short clock: the flush.** Every midnight, while the station still calls, water runs through the tunnel for one hour (six turns). One turn before, the hum quickens. During the flush:

| Where | What the water does |
|-------|---------------------|
| Reaches (the long plain tubes) | The floor channel overflows. Water a foot deep on the walkway. Half speed. |
| T2, the Gauge Hall | Fills to four feet: an elf's chest, over a gnome's head. Each turn a PC moves here, save vs. Paralysis or be knocked down and carried 30' west (1d4). A gnome swims or is carried. |
| T4, the Sweep | The sweep runs once every turn of the flush. |
| T1, T3, T5 | Wet floors. Nothing worse. |

A party that leaves the Fort at dawn and goes straight to the Weirs has fourteen hours before the flush. A party that visits both Boar Hollow and the milestone arrives at half past four with seven and a half hours, or 45 turns. Walking the reaches carefully and doing every room takes about 35. That is enough, unless they rest long, retreat, or linger in T3.

### Two Buyers

| | **The B.I.A.** (Mile-House 13) | **The Quiet** (the Weirs) |
|---|---|---|
| **Wants** | The tunnel sealed, the anomaly closed, and Agent Platt's map filed at Mile-House 13 | The water to keep running past their shrines |
| **Why** | An open anomaly is a form that is never finished. And they are frightened: their senior agent went in and did not come out. | Fish came back to the Weirs with the water, and the kingfishers came with the fish. To the Quiet that is a prayer answered. |
| **Pays** | 200 gp when the tunnel is shut and the map filed. 100 gp more for Agent Platt, brought home. ("Alive is preferred. There is a form either way.") | A string of river pearls (150 gp), when the water still runs on the fourth night. Fish for the Fort. |
| **Asks how** | The two Junior Men in Red, at the Fort, politely | With hands and cords, at the Weirs, in silence |

Neither buyer is wrong. The **Ending** below says what each choice gives and costs.

> **What This Session Teaches:** **Planning and Execution.** Talk is free; dice cannot be taken back. **Fate with a spell.** A Fate point spent with a spell or an N/day power lets the player write part of what it does. **Six-mile hexes.** A day is a budget of hours, and every hex has a price and a check. **The puzzle rule.** The math is the way through, not a lock on the story: try small numbers, listen to the feedback, and you can always walk on.

## Table Procedures

*Teach each rule the first time it matters, not before. The "First matters" column says when.*

| Rule | How it works | First matters |
|------|--------------|---------------|
| **Planning and Execution** | The DM says the mode out loud. **Planning:** talk, maps, Fort decisions, looking and asking. The DM answers honestly. No dice. No time passes. **Execution:** dice and consequences. A player cannot undo an Execution result. If a player acts during Planning ("I step on the slab"), ask once: "Is that your plan, or your action?" After that, it is an action. | At the Hall, with the route on the table. Then the first doorway: T1. |
| **Six-mile hexes** | One hex is 6 miles. Road: 2 hours. Forest: 3 hours. Reeds: 4 hours. (OSE: 24 miles a day at 120'; forest two-thirds; marsh half.) Check for an encounter once per hex entered, and once per night camped: d6, an encounter on a 1 on the road, 1–2 in forest, 1–3 in the reeds. Distance 4d6 × 10 yards. Surprise 1–2 on a d6. Off the road in forest, a party with no guide gets lost on 1–2 in 6: one hour lost and another check. | Leaving the Fort's gate. |
| **The turn** | In the tunnel, Module 2's Dungeon Clock: a turn is 10 minutes; explore 120' a turn; a torch burns 6 turns; check for wanderers every 2 turns (1-in-6); rest one turn in six. The plain reaches between rooms have their own rule (see **The Outflow Tunnel**). | T1. |
| **Fate** | 3 points each, refreshed at the start of each session and at each Fort Turn. **Spend 1:** reroll; +4 to a save after the roll; or state one true fact in your lane. **Spend 2:** cheat death. **Year Two:** spend 1 *with a spell or an N/day power* and the spell works, and the player also writes part of its effect. The DM may say "Tell me more" and shape it; the fact may add to the scene but never undoes what the DM has said. **Mutual bonds:** two cards whose bonds name each other give each player 1 extra Fate this session, spent only to help that partner. | The first spell cast, or the first fact declared. Prompt Fate abilities *before* you describe a place. |
| **Witnessed or not** | A Fate fact stated at the Fort, before Cassandra or the Terminal, or in front of the Quiet is witnessed: the Fort learns it, and it may become a public fact. A fact stated in the tunnel is unwitnessed: only the party knows it, unless they tell. | The first Fate fact. |
| **Traps** | Anyone who looks can see a trap's signs in Planning; each trap's table lists them. The **Read** line (the trigger and the safe way) comes from a turn of searching the right 10' square (1-in-6 each searcher), from the thief's Locksmith's Eye, from *I Was Already Here*, or from working it out aloud from the signs. Once read, anyone can spike or avoid it. The thief's Find/Remove Treasure Traps roll (15% at level 2) is for small traps on locks and chests, as the playbook says; room traps are searched for like anyone else. | The bell-line in T1. |
| **The puzzle rule** | A puzzle has a rule you can find by trying small numbers. It gives feedback. It never blocks the way on. One hint comes from a Fate point, from Cassandra, or from the Lorekeeper. | T3. |

> **Example: Fate with a spell.** At the Weirs, wolves come out of the reeds. The druid casts *Entangle* and spends a Fate point. The spell works as written. The player adds: "The reeds hold the wolves, and they lean over to point at the stone mouth in the bank." Now it is true, and the party knows where the mouth is without searching.

## What the DM Needs to Know

### The Outflow Station (the machine side, plainly)

The tunnel is imperial waterworks. It is the outflow of **Halcyon**, the drowned lamp-city under the lake, and one of the three ways in (the Isle stair, the Quiet's dive, and this tunnel). Halcyon is the Year Two DM Guide's to key. This session stops at its door.

| Piece | Mystery reading (brush) | Machine reading (lean in) |
|-------|------------------------|---------------------------|
| **The hum** | The milestone sings a new song. | A **signal**. The milestone is a relay post on the old line. The outflow station is sending a call for a maintenance crew: three, four, five, then long notes that count down. |
| **The flush** | Water comes at midnight. | Each night the station tests its sluice for one hour. |
| **The crew test** (T3) | A lamp that lights when the cord makes a true corner | Imperial crews laid out square corners with knotted cord. The station asks anyone who wants a **crew lamp** to do it once. It is a lock that asks you to prove you were trained. |
| **The crew lamp** | A lamp that burns without oil | A credential. The station's machines read it. The sweep (T4) stays parked, the Tender (T5) stands down, and the inner door's eye opens for it. |
| **The fail-safe** | The song ends. | If no crew answers by the fourth midnight, the station shuts the sluice, locks the inner door and stops calling. |

*This session's invention. If the Year Two DM Guide names the missing agent, use its name.*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Is** | The Junior Men's senior. Forty, red coat, ink on every finger. Polite. Wrong about many things: he believes the hum is "a Fae broadcast" and has a form for that. |
| **Did** | Came to survey the hum three weeks ago. Lodged at Boar Hollow. Went in once as far as T3, chalked his tries at the frame, gave up, came out and nailed his route map in the lodge, as Bureau procedure says. Went back in. Tripped the sweep in T4, broke a leg, and crawled into a crew niche. |
| **Is now** | In the south-east niche of T4. 1 hp (of 7). He has drunk flush water for three weeks and eaten his candles. He cannot cross the gallery on one leg before the sweep comes back. |
| **Knows** | The pan trips the sweep. It runs west, then back, then is ready again in a turn. The flush is at midnight. "A brass custodian" lives at the far end and carried him out once. He never solved the frame. |
| **Wants** | Out. The anomaly sealed and his map filed. He offers his purse (40 gp) to whoever carries him. |

### The Junior Men in Red

Two teenage agents from Mile-House 13 (F4). Use the Year Two DM Guide's names. Red coats too big for them, a satchel of forms, a brass Bureau whistle each. Polite and wrong about many things. They believe the hum comes from the Standing Stones (E5). They are not cleared for "subterranean work" and will not enter the tunnel. They want their senior back more than they will say.

### The Quiet

Lake-folk of the Reed Shore who pray to the kingfisher. They do not speak aloud. They answer with hands and knotted cords. Since the water came, three families have walked round the lake to the Weirs and built shrines along the stream: reed poles, each with a blue feather and a flat stone where they pour water at dawn. They will not enter the tunnel. They do not take sides in anything but the water.

### The Brush: Two Depths for Every Machine Element

Year Two tests whether the players want a science-fiction setting. The science fantasy **brushes** the Fort's world; it does not take it over. Every machine element in this session has two depths:

- **Brush:** seen, named, left alone. The mystery reading. It costs nothing and gives nothing extra.
- **Lean in:** opened, explained a little, with a reward. The machine reading.

**Reading the players.** Each Fort Card carries a **Lens**: *Hymn*, *Machine* or *Both*. Start every element at brush. Go to lean in when a player's Lens says so and that player looks, or when any player's questions ask for it ("What is it made of?" "Who built it?" "How does it know?"). Never push a Hymn player through a door they did not knock on. After the session, note which elements the table leaned into. If the cards and the table lean in, Year Two can turn toward science fiction. If not, it stays fantasy with old ruins.

| Element | Brush (seen, named, left) | Lean in (opened, explained a little) | Lean-in reward |
|---------|---------------------------|--------------------------------------|----------------|
| **The humming milestone** (C4) | A warm stone that sings in pulses: three, four, five, then long notes. | A relay post. An ear or the lake stone on it shows the pulses come from the north-east, from the Weirs. A bronze plate at its foot, under the moss, bears a wave and an arrow. | The 3-4-5 count, if Wick has not given it. The marker-stone path to the Weirs: an hour saved. |
| **The B.I.A.'s forms and gear** | Red coats, whistles, a satchel of forms, a chain for measuring. Funny and harmless. | The forms are printed, not written: every one the same, from a press nobody in the Fort country owns. The chain is imperial, marked in feet. Platt's map uses survey signs. | Platt's map read properly gives each reach's length in paces (see C5): the party can plan its time to the turn. |
| **The outflow tunnel** (the waterworks) | A long dry tunnel with old stone and lime. | Imperial waterworks: a channel in the floor, depth marks in T2, numbered stations, crew niches in T4. The tunnel was built to carry water out of somewhere under the lake. | Knowing it is a drain tells the party the flush comes down it: they read the weed line in T2 and plan around midnight. |
| **The drop gate** (T2) | A jaw of bronze teeth in the ceiling. | A flood gate on a counterweight, tripped by a pressure slab. | The thief or the druid jams it for good (spike, or *Warp Wood*). |
| **The frame and the crew lamp** (T3) | A lamp that lights when the cord makes a true corner, and a lamp that needs no oil. | A crew test. The lamp is a credential the station's machines can read. | The sweep and the Tender stand down. The inner door's eye opens. |
| **The sweep** (T4) | A stone blade that clears the gallery. | A debris plough on a pressure pan, with crew niches built in to dodge it. | Parked by the crew lamp. Platt comes out walking on two shoulders, not running. |
| **The Tender** (T5) | A brass creature with one lamp eye, curled in a wall. | A maintenance machine. It does not kill. It clears. | With the crew lamp, it obeys one simple order: carry, lift, fetch. |
| **The inner door** (T5) | A round door with a shut glass eye. Behind it, water moving far off. | The station's inner lock. Past it lies the Eval Chamber of Halcyon (DM Guide). The eye reads the crew lamp and answers the call. | The hum steadies to one note: the call is answered, the station will not give up, and the door will open next time. |

> **Design Note:** Every read-aloud in this file works at brush depth. The mystery comes first; the mechanism is there for whoever looks. That is the whole Brush in one rule.

## Part One: At the Fort

*Planning. No dice. This is where the gate is first taught: say "We're in Planning" when you open the scene, and "Execution" when they walk out of the gate.*

The intros all end at the same image: the milestone hums, and Cassandra says one true thing to each of them. This session begins the next morning, in the Hall.

> **Read aloud:** The Hall smells of bread and wet wool. On the council table Maren has laid out a slate of chalk marks, a bundle of reeds tied with blue thread, and a sheet of good paper with a red wax seal. At the end of the table, two young men in red coats too big for them stand up very straight. Somewhere outside, faint as a thought, the milestone is still humming.

| On the table | Who brought it | What it says |
|--------------|----------------|--------------|
| **The slate** | Wick, the lamplighter (12) | His count. *"Three, four, five, long. Every time. The long ones: seven, six, five, four. Tonight three."* He has drawn the long notes as a falling stair. Ask the players what comes after three. |
| **The reeds** | A Fort fisher | Cut at the Weirs. Blue thread is the Quiet's. "They've built shrines on the new water. Never seen them this side of the lake." |
| **The sealed letter** | The Junior Men in Red | A Bureau notice: Senior Agent Platt is overdue. "Procedure Nine: agents leave a route map at the last shelter. We do not know which shelter. The Bureau would be grateful." |
| **Word from Boar Hollow** | Tobin Ash, the hunter, by way of Harrow's shop | "A man in a red coat left a map nailed in my lodge and his pony in my pen. Three weeks. Somebody owes me for oats." |

**Maren's ask.** "Find where that water comes from and what the hum wants. Bring back the Bureau man if he's alive. Then tell me what we do about it, because the Bureau will ask, and so will the Quiet, and I'd like to hear your answer before theirs."

**The Junior Men's offer.** They read the public facts from their file, one wrong detail each, and wait to be corrected. Then: 200 gp when "the anomaly is sealed and Senior Agent Platt's route map is filed at Mile-House 13", and 100 gp for Platt. They will not go in. They will come to the Weirs on the second day.

**Cassandra.** If anyone visits the Shrine before leaving, she gives one true thing about the tunnel, half riddled. If a PC's bond names her, her puzzle hint goes to that PC (see T3). If a player has no line from the intro, use one of these:

| PC | Cassandra says |
|----|----------------|
| Thief | "You have walked there before. Count your steps, and step where the stone is pale." |
| Cleric | "Something under the water is asking for hands. Whose hands will you give it?" |
| Druid | "The kingfisher fishes only where the water moves. Ask her why it moves at night." |

> **What This Scene Teaches:** **Planning is free.** The slate, the reeds, the letter, the map in the lodge: everything here is for looking at and talking about. The players choose a route. Nothing happens until they say "We go", and then you say "Execution."

### C4. The Humming Milestone

A stone post as tall as a man, warm to the touch. Its hum is a note you feel in your teeth. A stone lid at its foot hides a hollow. (If your table played Module 3, the hollow is as that module left it.) Since a month ago the hum comes in pulses: three, four, five, then long notes.

| If the party | Then |
|--------------|------|
| Listens for a full turn | Anyone can count it: three, four, five, long. |
| Puts an ear, a hand or the lake stone on it | **Lean in.** The pulses come from the north-east. The Lake Stone tingles. Under the moss at the foot, a bronze plate shows a wave and an arrow pointing north-east: the marker stones. |
| Spends Fate (*The Archives Are Deep*) | A fact about the old line: "Every milestone on this road was a relay for the waterworks." Make it matter in T3 or T5. |

| The hum | Changes with every corner the cord makes. That is the feedback. |

### The Puzzle: True Corners

**What the players do.** Loop the cord round three pegs, starting and ending at a lamp peg, so it makes a triangle. The two sides that touch the lamp peg are its **arms**. The side that does not touch it is the **far side**. Players say three numbers: arm, arm, far side. Each must be a whole number of spaces, and the three must add up to the cord.

**The rule (for the DM).** Square each arm and add. Compare with the far side squared.

| The cord makes | The DM says |
|----------------|-------------|
| No corner: the far side is as long as the two arms together, or longer | "The cord lies flat. The lamp blinks once." |
| A true corner: arm² + arm² = far² | "The lamp lights and stays lit. The hum steadies to one note." |
| A sharp corner: arm² + arm² is more than far² | "The hum rises, thin." |
| A wide corner: arm² + arm² is less than far² | "The hum falls, low." |

Squares, for the DM: 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, 64, 81, 100, 121, 144, 169, 196, 225, 256.

**The answers.**

| Frame | Cord | True corner | What it gives |
|-------|------|-------------|---------------|
| Little frame | 12 | **3, 4, 5** (arms 3 and 4, far side 5) | A chime. The little lamp glows for a turn. Nothing else. It is the lesson. |
| Great frame | 36 | **9, 12, 15** (three little cords: the red knots mark them) | The crew lamp lights and lifts off its peg. |
| The locker | 30 | **5, 12, 13** (not a bigger 3-4-5: the real rule) | The locker opens: the crew's pay. |

**Trying small numbers.** The little cord has only three ways to make a triangle: 4-4-4, 2-5-5 and 3-4-5, in some order. A table that tries them all finds 3-4-5 in a minute, and finds that the long side must be the far one. The milestone's count was three, four, five. Wick's slate says the same. The great cord is three little cords long, and the red knots show where. The locker will not take a bigger 3-4-5 (30 is not a whole number of twelves), so the players must find the rule itself, or try. Its feedback guides them: 6-10-14 falls; 8-10-12 rises; 5-12-13 lights.

**The hint path.** One hint from each source the party has.

| Source | The hint |
|--------|----------|
| **Cassandra** (given before leaving, as a question, to the PC bonded to her or to whoever asked) | "Must a corner's two arms be the same length?" |
| **The Lorekeeper** (free, once) | "Solve the little frame first. Then count the red knots." |
| **A Fate point** (any PC, once) | "The long side is never an arm. And the big cord is the little one, three times." |

**The Fate-point shortcut.** A PC uses a Fate ability (one of its free uses, or 1 Fate point after those) and states a fact in their lane, and it is true: the cleric, *"The archives say imperial crews trued corners with a cord of three, four and five, and the stations test the same."* The thief, with *I Was Already Here*: *"I counted the pegs last time: nine along, twelve up."* The druid's *The Land Speaks* does not work here (the room is built, not grown) unless the druid is level 3. The lamp lights. No cost but the Fate. The math is the way through, not a lock on the story.

**It never blocks.** The east exit is open. A party that walks on without the crew lamp can still pass T4 and T5; it is only harder. Platt did exactly that.

**The locker without the puzzle.** Open Locks (20% at level 2), or force it: 3 turns, loud, a wanderer check each turn.

**In the locker:** 60 imperial gold coins (5 gp each, 300 gp): crew pay that was never paid. A bronze slate: the station's crew roster, eight names, the last marked *absent*.

> **What This Room Teaches:** **The puzzle rule.** A rule you can find by trying small numbers. Feedback on every try: the hum rises, falls, or holds. It never blocks the way. One hint each from Cassandra, the Lorekeeper and a Fate point. And a ceiling for the player who wants one: the locker.

> **Design Note:** Give the players a real string with 13 knots an inch apart, a string with 37 knots, and a sheet of squared paper. Let them lay it out on the table and check the corner with the edge of a book. Platt's chalk is a worked example of trying and failing: he only tried equal arms. Obvious: lay the cord, see what happens. Clever: read Platt's chalk first and notice what he never tried. Brush: the lamp lights when the corner is true, and a lamp that needs no oil comes away in your hand. Lean in: it is a crew test, and the lamp is a credential; tell the Machine-Lens player that the station's other machines will know it.

> **Art reference:** Otus ink, black on cream. From above: the peg field as a grid of dots, a white cord stretched in a right triangle across it, one figure crouched at each corner holding the cord down, and at the lamp peg a small circle of light drawn as bare paper among the hatching.

### The Tender

It wakes if anyone touches the door, the wheel or the grate while no crew lamp is in the chamber. It unfolds, lights its one lamp eye, and clears debris. To the Tender, the party is debris.

| AC | HD (hp) | Att | THAC0 | MV | SV | ML | XP |
|----|---------|-----|-------|----|----|----|----|
| 3 [16] | 3* (15) | 2 × hook (1d6) | 17 [+2] | 60' (20') | D12 W13 P14 B15 S16 (3) | 12 | 50 |

*5e: CR 2 · AC 16 · hp 15 · 2 hooks +2 (1d6 each) · both hit: grappled and dragged 20' a round · immune to sleep and charm.*

It does not kill. If both hooks hit, it carries its catch south down the tube toward the T4 sump at 20' a round. It lets go if it takes 5 or more damage in one round, or if the catch breaks free (1–2 on a d6). Immune to *sleep*, charm and *hold*. *Heat Metal* cooks its works: it stops after the third round of the spell. Moving silently past it while it is folded is possible (Quiet Feet helps). With the crew lamp in the chamber it does not wake; shown the lamp while awake, it folds back up. With the lamp, it obeys one simple spoken order a turn: carry, lift, fetch, open the grate.

### The Stop

When the party has seen the door and made or put off the choice at the wheel, read this and end the session.

> **Read aloud:** The hum holds one note, or it goes on pulsing; either way, it is the only sound. Behind the round door the water moves again, closer than before, and then away. Whatever is on the other side has heard you come this far. It does not open. Not tonight.

> **What This Room Teaches:** **The end of a road is a choice, not a fight.** The Tender is the fight for a party that comes without the lamp. For a party that comes with it, the room is a question: what do we do with the water?

> **Design Note:** Obvious: fight the Tender, try the door, turn the wheel. Clever: bring the lamp; or leave the wheel alone and go home to ask the Fort. Do not open the door. Do not describe what is past it. The Elf Cleric's Lake Stone tingles strongly here; say so and nothing more. Brush: a round door with a shut eye, and water behind it. Lean in: the station's inner lock, which reads the crew lamp and answers the call.

> **Art reference:** Elmore paint, the session's last image. The round bronze door fills the frame. Three small figures before it, one holding up a lamp whose pale light falls on the glass eye as its lid begins to open. The Tender half unfolded in its alcove at the left. Warm bronze and cold white light; the rest in deep green-black.

## The Ending: Who Gets the Water

The party may choose at the wheel, or walk home and choose at the Hall. None of the choices is correct. Do not steer.

| Choice | What they do | What it gives | What it costs |
|--------|--------------|---------------|---------------|
| **Shut and file** (the B.I.A.) | Turn the wheel to *shut* (or force and jam it), chain the grille at T1 with Bureau seals, and file Platt's map at Mile-House 13. | 200 gp. The Bureau's thanks in writing: Mile-House 13 will answer one request a season. | The water stops tonight. The fish leave the Weirs. The Quiet take down their shrines and turn their backs on the Fort. One of Halcyon's three ways in is shut. |
| **Let it run** (the Quiet) | Leave the wheel at *open* (and, without the lamp, wedge it so the station cannot shut it), say nothing to the Bureau, or tell them it is "a drain". | The Quiet's pearls (150 gp). Fish at the Weirs. A cord with one knot: the Quiet will guide the Fort once on the lake. | The Bureau files a complaint, politely, then sends an inspector. An open way under the Fort's country that the Fort must watch. |
| **Hold the lamp** (the Fort) | Answer the call with the crew lamp, leave the wheel unlocked, take the lamp home, and promise each buyer an answer later. | The crew lamp, at the Fort. The water runs or stops on the Fort's word. | Both buyers keep asking. Neither pays. |
| **Walk away** | Do nothing, or come too late. On the fourth midnight the station gives up. | Nothing. | The water stops. The hum stops. The Bureau files "self-resolved" and pays nothing. If Platt is still in T4, he dies when his water stops. |

The Bureau pays 100 gp for Platt brought home alive whatever the party chooses. "Procedure."

> **Design Note:** A Steward sees the tracks for each choice before choosing; show them **The Fort Reacts** below. Platt, if rescued, argues for shutting it. The Quiet, if met, have already made their offer. Let both be heard. Then let the players decide.
