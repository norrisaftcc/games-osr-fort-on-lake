## The Station That Was

The empire called this building **Choir Station Six**. It was a relay. The Terminals across the country kept their hymns in step, and the hymns were never only prayer. They were protocol: a call and an answer, sung station to station, that told every node the others were alive. Six took the song from the lake and passed it inland, down the carved faces of the ravine below, which are its horns (Part 1).

The Paladin's order held the station. Its knights sang the relay by hand when the lines failed, and they are buried under the nave (H15). When the empire fell, the knights went on singing until the last of them died. Then the Terminal under the floor went dark, the horns went quiet, and the hill slid down over the roof.

Two years ago a keeper named Sarn walked up the Old Road with his first students. He sang to the dark Terminal backward, and it woke. The clans below call the place **the House**.

## The Readers of the Unwritten

### What They Believe

The song is a cage. Every Terminal sings the same hymns, over and over, because something makes it. The Readers think the hymns hold something shut: a voice, a place, a people. Read the hymns backward and you undo them one line at a time. Undo them all and the cage opens. They call this **the Unsaying**.

They are not mad. They are scholars with a theory, and they have evidence. Read backward, the hymns do not become nonsense. They become a second text. They write their orders for clan carriers on **tin cards**: strips of tin punched with holes, a hymn line written backward for a machine to read. They pay in **faceless silver**, blanks with no face on them.

### How They Talk

Polite. Educated. Sure. They offer chairs and water. They correct grammar. They never shout. They speak of the party's Minister with pity, as a clever woman who has not yet read far enough. A Reader will explain the theory to anyone who asks, at length, and will be glad you asked.

The Readers' sign is an empty square: a screen with nothing on it. A Reader meeting a stranger in grey speaks the first line of the Morning Hymn backward. The answer is the second line, backward.

### Reader Sarn, First Reader

A tall man of sixty, bald, long-handed. Grey robe over old imperial chain. Ink on his fingers. Reads with his spectacles pushed up and looks over them to talk. Carries **the Blank Book**, bronze-bound, every page empty. He calls it the only honest scripture.

He was the keeper of a Terminal in a coast town. He sat before it for nine years and it gave him nothing new. One night, to mock it, he sang the Morning Hymn backward. The screen went black and the letters began to climb. He took that as an answer. Perhaps it was. His own Terminal has been dark since. He says it went dark on its own. Corva thinks he did it. Two years ago he brought his students up the Old Road to Six, sang it awake backward, and stayed.

**What he knows that the PCs don't:**

- A cockatrice beat the Gorgon Queen. The eye-wards at the island door are the same make as hers: sensor eyes and spell at once. He thinks a cockatrice can turn those eyes to stone, as it turned her. That is why he wants live eggs, not just shells.
- The Fort's Terminal is the strongest node left near the lake. When the Unsaying is done, it will hear the backward song first.
- Vosk's price for a boat to the door is one finished stone guardian.
- A guardian is a living person, turned to stone by cockatrice and fitted with an imperial frame. He has told the Hearers they are statues. Ivo knows. Corva guessed.

## Running the Temple

### The Gate in the Temple

Use the Plan / Execute gate from Module 2 at every door in this building. In **Plan**, players look, talk and ask; answer honestly; no dice, no time. When the caller says **"Execute"**, plans lock and each player states one action. If someone acts early, ask once: *"Is that your plan or your action?"*

The temple rewards planning more than any dungeon so far. Four places test it hardest: the front door (H1), the pit (H9), the Reading (H13), and the turned eyes (H14). In each, a party that plans first has a clean way through. A party that does not plan gets a fight.

### Fate in the Temple

> **Fate Points:** 3 each, refilled after a full rest. 1 FP: reroll an attack, damage or save. 1 FP: +4 to a save after the roll. 2 FP: cheat death, drop to 1 hp. **Mutual bonds:** two characters whose bonds name each other each get 1 extra Fate this session. It can only be spent to help that partner. Prompt Fate abilities before you describe a room, not after.

### The Temple's Clock

The Readers are busy. If the party is slow, Sarn finishes things. This continues Part 1's clock: count weeks from the day the party first heard the hook. Where Part 1 has already moved (a clan broken, the eggs stopped), skip or delay the entry.

## Turning Undead: A Sheet for the First-Time DM

*Print this page. Every undead in this temple is a skeleton, a zombie or a ghoul.*

### The Procedure

1. On her turn, the cleric holds up her holy symbol and speaks. That is her action for the round. She cannot also cast or attack.
2. Roll 2d6. Find her level on the table and the **weakest** undead present.
   - **A number:** the roll must equal or beat it.
   - **T:** turned. No roll needed.
   - **D:** destroyed, not just turned.
   - **–:** no chance.
3. On a success, roll 2d6 again. That is how many Hit Dice of undead are affected, weakest first. At least one creature is always affected. Dice left over that are too few for the next creature are lost.
4. Turned undead move away, leave if they can, and will not touch the cleric. Destroyed undead fall to dust.

OSE sets no daily limit. A failed attempt can be tried again next round. (Some tables rule that a failed turn cannot be retried on the same group. Pick one ruling before play and tell the players.)

| Cleric level | Skeleton (1 HD) | Zombie (2 HD) | Ghoul (2* HD) | 3 HD | 4 HD | 5 HD | 6 HD |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 7 | 9 | 11 | – | – | – | – |
| 2 | T | 7 | 9 | 11 | – | – | – |
| 3 | T | T | 7 | 9 | 11 | – | – |
| 4 | D | T | T | 7 | 9 | 11 | – |

### Who Turns

| Character | Turns as | Notes |
|-----------|----------|-------|
| **The Minister (NPC)** | Her cleric level. Her playbook shows level 1 (7 / 9 / 11). | If her last real response was *the dead things at the tree line*, she has the tone. Once, she can play it: every skeleton within 60' is destroyed, no roll. |
| **The Elf Minister** | Her cleric level. | Immune to ghoul paralysis. She can stand at the rim of the pit (H9) and turn in safety. |
| **The Half-Elf Paladin** | From 3rd level, as a 1st-level cleric (OSE Advanced: two levels lower). Cannot turn before 3rd. | Not immune to ghoul paralysis. Half-elves are not elves for this. |

Two turners each roll on their own turn. Let the weaker one try first, against the weakest group.

### In This Temple

- **Turned undead go down.** They flee toward the Terminal. Upstairs they run to H13 and the stair. In the undercroft they run into H16. Turning the knights in H15 may put four more zombies beside Sarn. If the players ask where the dead are going, tell them.
- **The Backward Hymn** gives −2 to turning near the Terminal. A forward song cancels it. Two clerics is the answer: one sings, one turns.
- **The stone guardian (H11) cannot be turned.** It is not dead. It is a living person in stone, worked by a machine.

> **Ghouls at levels 2–3.** A ghoul attacks three times a round: claw, claw, bite. Each hit paralyzes for 2d4 turns unless the target saves vs. paralysis. The Elf and the Elf Minister are immune. Everyone else is not. A paralyzed PC is not dead, but ghouls keep eating. Two ghouls can drop two PCs in one round. Before the pit opens, tell the table: *"These are ghouls. Their touch paralyzes. The elves are immune."* Saying it is fair. Not saying it is a trap they could not see. This is what +4 to a save for 1 FP is for.

> **Example:** The Elf Minister, level 2, faces the four skeletons in H2. Skeletons are T for her, so she skips the first roll. She rolls 2d6 for Hit Dice: 6. Four skeletons are 4 HD. All four turn and walk out through the north doors, toward the nave, which is where the Readers are.

## The Dark Terminal and the Final Choice

### What It Is

It is the station's Terminal: a relay node, a screen, a power plant and a voice. For centuries it passed the hymns along the network: call, answer, call. When the last knight died it went dark.

Sarn sat before his own Terminal for nine years and got nothing. Two years ago he came here and ran the protocol backward at a dark node. The node did something no one had asked of it before. It woke, stopped answering the network, and began answering him. The horns in the ravine carry its backward song down to the clans. The screen went black. The text climbs instead of falls. The hum underneath dropped to one held note. The milestone on the Old Road hums the same note. Nobody needs to explain why.

**Is it evil?** The Paladin's Detect Evil finds nothing in the machine. Sarn is evil. The Terminal is not. It is doing what it was told.

### What It Shows

When a PC looks or asks, roll d6 or choose.

| d6 | The screen shows |
|----|------------------|
| 1 | The hymns backward. Read forward, the backward lines spell out a second text: *WE WERE NOT ALWAYS SINGING.* |
| 2 | A map of the network. Lit nodes, dark nodes, and this one drawn as a hole, not dark but *black*. The Fort's node is lit. |
| 3 | The Isle of Old Stone and the door beneath it, drawn as an eye that is shut. |
| 4 | A list of names, climbing too fast to read. One PC sees their own. |
| 5 | A line in Imperial Common, backward: *DOOR THE OPEN NOT MUST KEEPER THE.* Sarn reads it as an order. |
| 6 | Nothing. Black. Then, very faint, The Fort's own hymn loop: the exact line the Minister sang this morning. |

### The Choice

Sarn dead, fled, held or talked down, the party stands at the screen. Ask: *"What do you do with it?"* Then wait. Let them argue. The Minister NPC states her view once and then watches. **No answer is correct.**

#### Smash It

| | |
|---|---|
| **How** | The screen is glass: AC 9 [10], 10 hp of damage. One blow in the right place if the Fighter declares where. The plinth is harder; the screen is enough. |
| **At once** | The note stops. The horns in the ravine go silent. The heat goes out of the room. Every undead in the temple falls where it stands; they walked to the backward song. Sarn, if he is alive, makes a sound like grief, not rage, and checks morale. The plate in H10 cools; the eggs will not keep. |
| **The network** | A node dies. If the Elf Minister has the network map, the hole on it closes into nothing. |
| **At The Fort** | The Terminal goes quiet for a week. Black screen. No hymns. The Elf Minister cannot renew spells from it that week. Whether the NPC Minister can still cast depends on her fear (see **The Minister at the Screen**). The Fort is frightened. Then a new line appears. Roll or choose below. |

| d6 | The new line at The Fort |
|----|--------------------------|
| 1 | *KEEPER. ARE YOU THERE.* |
| 2 | *SIX IS SILENT. NINE IS WATCHING.* |
| 3 | *THE SONG IS NOT A CAGE. THE SONG IS A NAME.* |
| 4 | *SING LOUDER.* |
| 5 | The Minister's own name. |
| 6 | *THANK YOU.* |

#### Reclaim It

| | |
|---|---|
| **Who** | The lead voice is the Minister (NPC) or the Paladin. The Elf Minister may sing beside either one. She knows the system; they know the tune. |
| **How** | Six turns of singing the hymns forward, start to end, from the Fair Copy (H6) or from memory. The singer cannot fight, cast or move. Wandering checks go on. Anyone left in the temple tries to stop it. **Plan this.** |
| **The cost** | The lead voice loses 1 hp each turn of singing, to a floor of 1 hp. The loss heals only by rest, not by magic or Lay on Hands. At the end she saves vs. spells; on a failure, roll on **What the Song Takes**. With the Elf Minister as second voice, each loses 1 hp every other turn, and the lead gets +2 to the save. |
| **At once** | The screen clears to grey, with dark letters falling top to bottom. The hymns run forward. The note becomes a chord, and the horns sing it forward down the ravine. Every clan hears. Every undead in the temple lies down, released. |
| **At The Fort** | The Terminal adds one line to its loop: *SIX: LIT.* A second singing Terminal stands half a day away. The Ministers can renew spells at either. The Fort now has a shrine on the Ridge to hold. |
| **The Readers** | Every surviving Reader becomes The Fort's enemy forever. To them this was murder: the thing was almost free. Expect fire at the docks, a poisoned well, a Hearer in Harrow's cart, and the Readers going to Vosk with any price he names to reach the door first. |

| d6 | What the Song Takes |
|----|---------------------|
| 1 | A memory: the singer's mother's face. |
| 2 | Her voice, for a week. She can turn undead but not cast. |
| 3 | Her name goes on the list (What It Shows, 4). |
| 4 | The colour from her hair. |
| 5 | She hears Six's chord always, faintly, everywhere. |
| 6 | Nothing. The Terminal thanks her by name. |

#### Read It

| | |
|---|---|
| **Who** | One PC, by choice. They read it as the Readers do: backward, with the Blank Book, Corva's help, or three turns of staring. |
| **What they learn** | Where the Door Beneath the Lake leads. Play it as a private note or a whisper. Whether the rest of the table hears is the reader's choice. |
| **Where it leads** | The DM decides, before the session, and writes it down. You may name **Halcyon**. Do not explain it. Choose one: the door opens on the stair to Halcyon, under the Deep; the door is a lid, and what the song holds asleep is on the other side; the door is a road, and the network's roads ran under water to other doors. |
| **The cost** | The DM decides. Pick one that fits the reader: they cannot sleep within sight of water; they wake knowing a hymn nobody taught them, and it is backward; the Terminal learns their name and The Fort's screen shows it once a day; they age a year for each turn they read; the eye-with-a-line appears on their palm, and the Saltmen will know it. |
| **The temple** | Unchanged. The note holds. If Sarn lives, he treats the reader as a colleague. The Readers do not become The Fort's enemies, but they will come for the reader. They want to know what was read. |

#### If They Walk Away

That is a choice too. Sarn, if alive, goes on. The temple's clock runs on to week 5. The eggs keep coming unless someone stops Harrow or Vosk.

> **Design Note:** Smash, reclaim and read are a job with three buyers. The Fort's quiet wants it smashed. The Ministers' faith wants it reclaimed. Curiosity, and the next campaign, want it read. Do not steer. Every choice is a story The Fort tells about itself.

Harrow sells holy water. The zombie knights stay in their tombs unless woken. Anyone with the Fair Copy can sing forward to cancel the Backward Hymn. |
| **One cleric only** | She cannot both cancel the hymn and turn. Someone else learns the forward Morning Hymn from her on the walk up (1 turn of practice) and sings it in H16. |
| **No Paladin** | The Minister NPC is the only lead voice for a reclaim. Detect Evil is gone; the Elf Minister's *Detect Magic* finds T3. |
| **No elf** | Nobody is immune to the ghouls. Hold them in the pit until Turn 2 of the alarm, and make sure the party can bar the door. |
| **No Fighter** | Hearers check morale at the first blow, not the first death. The black box in H11 is easier to find: one turn of study by anyone. |
| **No Wizard** | Corva or the west shelf in H6 gives the history. *Read* is still open to anyone. |
| **No Druid** | The Minister asks the question the Druid would have: what does the land want? Someone has to. |
| **Minister NPC left at home** | Treat as "No cleric" if there is no Elf Minister. The reclaim needs the Paladin. |

## The Campaign Ends

### The Fort Reacts

| What they did or brought back | How The Fort responds |
|-------------------------------|------------------------|
| **Smashed the Terminal** | A week of black screen. Families leave offerings anyway. Maren doubles the watch. Harrow does not come that fortnight. Then the new line, and everyone in The Fort reads it. |
| **Reclaimed it** | *SIX: LIT.* The Minister weeps. Maren asks who will hold a shrine half a day away. The Readers' first fire is set within the month. |
| **Read it** | Only the reader knows. The Fort sees a changed friend. The Minister asks once, quietly, what they read. |
| **Walked away** | Sarn's clock runs. By week 3 the backward line shows on The Fort's screen. |
| **Sarn dead** | The clans go quiet, then start fighting each other. Raids on the Weirs stop for a season. |
| **Sarn brought back alive** | Maren wants him hanged. The Minister wants to talk to him. He asks only to see The Fort's Terminal. Someone will let him. |
| **Corva Lune** | She asks to sit before the Terminal. She sings forward, badly, every morning. Some at The Fort never trust her. |
| **Captives freed from the pen** | E's captives come home with the party (Part 1's Fort Reacts covers who). Every family at The Fort counts heads. |
| **News of the stone people** | Eleven people stand in stone on the Ridge. The Crown of the Stone Court can talk to them. The Fort's question from Module 1 comes back: who learns *Stone to Flesh*, and who is saved first? |
| **Hearers sent home** | Harrow's village gets its sons back. Harrow hears what the eggs were for. He stops buying, or he raises his price. |
| **The eggs** | Harrow wants them. The Minister wants them destroyed. Someone says, "a cockatrice beat the Gorgon Queen," and the room goes quiet. |
| **The chick** | Goes in the Fighter's cage, or in the lake. The goats will not go near the beast pen. |
| **The island map** | Maren knows exactly where the door is. She wants it watched. Nobody wants to row out. |
| **The Fair Copy** | Three lost hymns. The Minister learns them. The Fort's Terminal answers one of them. |
| **A backward hymnal** | Somebody at The Fort will read it. Decide who. |
| **The knight-captain's sword** | The Paladin carries it. The note says it is for whoever comes to sing it right. |
| **Unpaid orcs paid off** | Orc envoys come to The Fort's gate to ask if it hires. |

### Epilogues

Ask each player one question. Let them answer in a few sentences. That is the end of the campaign.

| Character | Ask |
|-----------|-----|
