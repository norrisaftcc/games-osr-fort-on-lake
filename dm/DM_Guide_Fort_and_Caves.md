# Dungeon Master's Guide

## *The Fort & The Petrified Caves*

---

# Part One: The Fort

## What This Section Is For

The Player's Guide tells them what their characters know. This tells you what's actually going on, what the NPCs want, and where the pressure points are. Skim it, know the NPCs, improvise from there.

OSE procedures handle exploration, time, encounters, and combat. Trust the book. This guide handles what the book can't: what people want, what's secret, and what happens when the players push on things.

---

## The Fort as a Living Place

The Fort has about fifty people and a problem: it's too small to be safe and too established to abandon. Every NPC is trying to answer the same question the PCs are — *what are we willing to do to survive?* — and they've each already decided. The tension comes from those decisions colliding.

---

## NPC Reference

### Captain Maren

| | |
|---|---|
| **Wants** | Security. A defensible position, reliable food, enough armed bodies on the wall. |
| **Will do** | Almost anything. Trade with people she doesn't trust, use resources she doesn't understand, send people into danger. |
| **Won't do** | Abandon The Fort. She's committed. She'd rather die here than run. |
| **Treats PCs as** | Assets. Glad they're here. Gives jobs, information, support. Won't baby them. |

**What she knows that the PCs don't:**

- The militia is undermanned. Two of six refuse to go near the ridge. She's down to four.
- She's been rationing quietly. Food is fine for now; one bad month and they're in trouble.
- She's seen the coast path bootprints herself. She thinks they're scouts, not bandits. Scouts mean someone is evaluating The Fort.

### The Minister (NPC Version)

If no player chose the Minister playbook, she's your NPC. Use the NPC Minister playbook for stats, secret, and Fate ability.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Role in fiction** | The conscience. Asks the hard question the PCs are avoiding. |
| **Role in dungeon** | Comes along because Petra was her friend (won't say this). Heals, turns undead, reacts to PC declarations. |
| **Key decision** | Pick her fear before session 1 and play toward it. |

### Old Harrow

| | |
|---|---|
| **Wants** | Profit and information, in that order. |
| **Treats PCs as** | Friendly, curious, careful. Good source of rumors. Won't lie, but will omit things bad for business. |

**What he knows that the PCs don't:**

- The fishing village is struggling too. He doesn't advertise this.
- He's heard rumors of other outposts going silent — not destroyed, just quiet.
- He'll buy cockatrice eggs at very good prices and won't say who they're for.

### Harrow's Shop

| Item | Price | Stock |
|------|-------|-------|
| Rope, 50' | 1 gp | Always |
| Torches (6) | 1 gp | Always |
| Rations (1 week) | 5 gp | Usually |
| Lamp oil (3 flasks) | 3 gp | Usually |
| Healing potion | 50 gp | Rare, 1 |
| Mirror, small steel | 5 gp | 1 |
| Holy water (1 vial) | 25 gp | Next trip |
| Arrows (20) | 5 gp | Yes |
| Shield, wooden | 10 gp | 1 |

> **Design Note:** The steel mirror costs 5gp and is the trick solution for the basilisk boss fight. Players who buy it are rewarded. Players who don't have to improvise. Either way works.

### The Militia

The two who went to the caves — Donal and Fen — remember walking up the ridge, entering a cave, and then nothing clear. Flashes. Cold. Something looking at them. They woke up on the trail a mile from the caves with Petra gone. They'll talk to PCs who seem competent, especially over a drink.

### The Beast Pen

The playbooks reference it — the Fighter built the cage — but the contents are deliberately open. Choose what creates the most interesting Premise pressure when the PCs return from the caves:

| Option | Implication |
|--------|-------------|
| A young cockatrice (hooded) | The PCs learn what's in the cage after fighting adults in the caves. What now? |
| A goblin (terrified, poor Common) | Caught stealing from snare lines. It knows about the caves. It won't go near them. |
| Something strange | A fox with too many eyes. A bird that speaks a dead language. A stone that hums. |

## Pressure Points

These create drama without forcing it. They're already in motion.

| Pressure | Status | Trajectory |
|----------|--------|------------|
| Food supply | Fine now | Tight by next month. Disruptions become urgent fast. |
| Militia strength | 4 of 6 reliable | PCs are the closest thing to reinforcements. Maren knows this. |
| Coast path strangers | Observing | Slow burn. Reward investigation with clues, not answers. |
| The Terminal | Looping | The Minister's relationship with it is The Fort's emotional center. |
| Lake island | Unexplored | Future adventure. NPCs are evasive in ways that increase curiosity. |

---

## After Adventures: The Fort Reacts

When PCs return, The Fort mirrors their choices. Don't plan outcomes in advance — react.

| What They Bring Back | How The Fort Responds |
|---------------------|----------------------|
| Cockatrice eggs | Harrow wants to buy. Maren wants threat assessment. Minister wants to know if they're connected to the Terminal. Families want to know if their children are safe. |
| Crown of the Stone Court | A magic item that lets you talk to petrified people. Petra is petrified. Implications are immediate and uncomfortable. |
| A cured Petra | She's back. She remembers. She's not the same. What she says about the queen's chamber depends on what you want the next adventure to be. |
| News: basilisk killed | Caves are safer. But the wards went dark. What else were they holding? |
| News: basilisk petrified | Wards still glow. She's contained, not destroyed. A different kind of problem. |

# Part Two: The Petrified Caves

## Overview

| | |
|---|---|
| **Location** | Northwest of The Fort, 3 hours through forest |
| **Structure** | Five-room dungeon |
| **Play time** | 2–3 hours |
| **Party** | 3–4 PCs plus the Minister (NPC or PC) |
| **Difficulty** | Dangerous. A PC can die or be petrified. That's a feature. |

---

## The Approach

Three hours through forest. The Hunter leads. Establish marching order and light sources before they enter.

The last half-mile: trees thin, undergrowth dies, ground turns rocky limestone. The air smells mineral. Cave mouth is fifteen feet wide, seven-foot ceiling. Cold air flows out.

> **Design Note:** Prompt Fate abilities before entry. The Thief's "I Was Already Here" or the Hunter's "I've Seen the Signs" can establish a fact about the approach. This teaches players that Fate abilities work *before* the DM describes things, not after.

---

## Room 1: The Garden of Stone

| | |
|---|---|
| **Size** | 30' × 20' |
| **Light** | Total darkness |
| **Exits** | North to Room 2 |
| **Encounter** | None |

> **Read aloud:** Six stone figures stand in the room, scattered as if caught mid-step. A petrified deer. A fox. Two armored figures in an old style. A woman holding a basket. A child. None of them are Petra.

The child's face is calm. Whatever happened was fast.

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| Stone figures | *Detect Magic*: faint transmutation on each. Armored figures carry stone weapons — old soldiers. Wizard's Fate ability can establish who. |
| Pressure plate | Center of room. Find Traps or 1-in-6. Triggers grinding sound deeper in. No damage, but Room 3's cockatrices are now awake and aggressive. |
| Stone apple | In the woman's basket. Worth 25gp. Taking it feels wrong. |

> **What This Room Teaches:** Look before you walk. Check for traps. The dungeon has a story and paying attention gives you advantages.

---

## Room 2: The Mirror Pool

| | |
|---|---|
| **Size** | 40' × 30', natural cavern, uneven floor |
| **Light** | Faint phosphorescence from pool (10' radius) |
| **Exits** | North (wide) to Room 3. East (narrow squeeze) to Room 4. |

> **Read aloud:** A still pool of water fills the center of the cavern, roughly fifteen feet across. Mirror-flat. No ripples, even when touched. The ceiling is thick with stalactites.

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| Pool reflection | Delayed by half a second. Unsettling, not dangerous. Hand in water: very cold, reflection shows a different room (one-way view of Room 5 — describe domed ceiling, carved eyes, throne. Don't describe the basilisk or Petra yet). |
| Scratch marks | Near narrow passage. Something clawed through — or was dragged. |
| Medallion | Bottom of pool (reach into cold water). Bronze, same symbol as Terminal plinth. Worth nothing as money. |

### Encounter: 2 × Cave Lizard

| HD | AC | ATK | MV | ML | Save |
|----|-----|-----|-----|-----|------|
| 1 | 7 [12] | 1 × bite (1d4+1) | 60' | 7 | F1 |

Drinking from pool. Not hostile unless cornered or exit blocked. Quiet approach (Move Silently or cautious play) avoids fight. If fought, they flee toward Room 1. If killed when avoidable, the Minister notices. Says nothing.

> **What This Room Teaches:** Not everything is a fight. The environment has information. Choices have social consequences even without mechanical ones.

---

## Room 3: The Nesting Hall

| | |
|---|---|
| **Size** | 50' × 30', high ceiling (20'+), natural columns |
| **Light** | Total darkness |
| **Exits** | South to Room 2. North descends to Room 5. |

> **Read aloud:** The ceiling is thick with what looks like mud-dauber nests clustered around the stalactites. Some broken, some sealed. The floor is littered with small animal bones and bird skeletons. And feathers. Metallic feathers. Bronze-colored.

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| Cockatrice eggs | Stone-grey, warm. Three intact. 50gp each to Harrow. Potentially dangerous (do they hatch? — DM's call). |
| Bronze feathers | Faintly magical (transmutation). Material component for *Stone to Flesh*. This is the key to saving Petra. |
| Bones | 30gp in scattered coins from previous victims. |

### Encounter: 2 × Cockatrice

| HD | AC | ATK | MV | ML | Save |
|----|-----|-----|-----|-----|------|
| 5** | 6 [13] | 1 × beak (1d6 + petrification) | 90'/180' fly | 7 | F5 |

**If Room 1 trap triggered:** Awake, aggressive, on columns.
**If trap not triggered:** Roosting. Group Move Silently to sneak past. Failure wakes them.

**Petrification:** Save vs. Petrification on hit or turn to stone. *Sleep* won't work (too many HD). *Light* on a cockatrice's eyes could blind it (save negates). Cockatrices flee at half HP — protecting eggs, not fighting to the death.

> **Design Note:** If a PC gets petrified, that's okay. The feathers are in this room. The scroll is in Room 4. The problem and the solution are in the same dungeon. If it happens, the session becomes a rescue — more dramatic than a replacement character sheet.

> **What This Room Teaches:** Combat has permanent consequences. Paying attention earlier (the trap) changes this fight. Resources here are worth something back at The Fort.

---

## Room 4: The Squeeze (Secret/Optional)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Size** | 5' × 5' passage → 10' × 10' chamber |
| **Light** | Total darkness |
| **Access** | Narrow passage from Room 2. Remove armor and pack (1 turn each way). Fighter in chain can't fit wearing it. |
| **Encounter** | None |

A skeleton in robes, long dead, clutching a leather satchel. A wizard from a long time ago.

| Item | Detail |
|------|--------|
| Scroll of *Stone to Flesh* | Single use. The cure for petrification. |
| Journal | Readable with *Read Magic*. Describes cave system. Last entry: *"The queen is below the pool. She does not move. She does not eat. She watches. I think she has been watching since the empire fell. I think the empire knew she was here. I think that's why they built the road."* |
| Silver dagger | 1d4, +1 to hit, counts as magical. |

> **Design Note:** The Wizard's Fate ability fires naturally here: "I've read about this wizard. They were from the Academy of ___." The player names the academy. It's now canon.

> **What This Room Teaches:** Exploration is rewarded. Not every valuable thing is behind a fight. Splitting the party is risky but sometimes worth it.

---

## Room 5: The Queen's Chamber

| | |
|---|---|
| **Size** | 40' × 40', domed ceiling, smooth carved walls |
| **Light** | Faint blue-green phosphorescence (pool in Room 2 acts as skylight) |
| **Exits** | South to Room 3. No other exits. |

> **Read aloud:** The walls are carved. Not natural — shaped deliberately. Spiraling patterns. Eyes. Dozens of carved eyes. In the center of the room: a stone throne. Something is sitting on the throne.

**Petra** stands ten feet from the throne. Stone. Hand outstretched — reaching for the basilisk, or pushing it away.

The carved eyes are wards. The throne is old — older than The Fort, older than the empire. Something put the basilisk here on purpose.

### Boss: The Basilisk Queen

| HD | AC | ATK | MV | ML | Save |
|----|-----|-----|-----|-----|------|
| 6+1** | 4 [15] | 1 × bite (1d10) + gaze | 60' | 9 | F6 |

### Gaze Attack

At the start of each round, every character facing the basilisk saves vs. Petrification. Players choose each round:

| Option | Penalty | Save Required? |
|--------|---------|----------------|
| Face the basilisk | Attack normally | Yes |
| Avert eyes | -4 to hit | No |
| Fight blind | -6 to hit, needs spotter | No |
| Use a mirror | Basilisk saves or is petrified | No |

### Each PC's Moment

| PC | Best Play |
|----|-----------|
| **Fighter** | Stand in front. Avert eyes. Hold the line at -4. Everyone behind you lives because of this. |
| **Wizard** | *Light* on the basilisk's eyes (save negates). If it works: blinded 1d4+1 rounds, gaze suppressed. *Magic Missile* for reliable damage. *Sleep* doesn't work. |
| **Thief** | Backstab with averted eyes: net +0 (+4 backstab, -4 avert). If wizard blinds it: full +4, double damage. Wait for the opening. |
| **Hunter** | Bow from entrance. -4 averted but safe from melee. If blinded, fire freely. Ambush on first shot if hidden. |
| **Minister** | She walks toward Petra. Reaches for whatever answers — the Terminal, the place, or nothing. If NPC, your dramatic beat. If PC, the player's. |

### Outcomes

| Result | Consequence |
|--------|-------------|
| Basilisk killed | Gaze fades. Wards pulse and go dark. Petra stays stone — needs scroll or feather components. Other statues stay. |
| Basilisk petrified (mirror) | Wards stay lit. Minister says she can feel it in there. Contained, not destroyed. |
| PC petrified | Use scroll if found. If not, feathers are the component — party can seek the spell later. Session doesn't end. Stakes got real. |

### Loot

| Item | Detail |
|------|--------|
| Crown of the Stone Court | Circlet. AC +1. Once/day: speak to a petrified creature for one minute. They're aware in there. They can answer. |
| Imperial coins | 200gp around throne base. |
| The throne | Immovable. |

> **What This Room Teaches:** Boss fights reward tactical thinking and teamwork. Combining abilities (blind + backstab, hold the line + shoot from range) is how you survive. The aftermath of a fight matters as much as the fight itself.

## After the Caves

The party returns with information, loot, and decisions to make. Don't script this. React.

### Questions the Players Must Answer

| Question | Stakes |
|----------|--------|
| Can Petra be saved? | Scroll (if found, not used) or feathers as component for *Stone to Flesh* (a spell nobody at The Fort knows yet — must be found). Crown lets you talk to Petra first. |
| What about the eggs? | Sell to Harrow (no questions). Destroy (Minister may prefer). Keep (dangerous). Hatch (insane, but someone will suggest it). |
| Is the queen dead or contained? | Killed: wards dark, what else were they doing? Petrified: wards glow, she's waiting. |
| Caves and Terminal connection? | Medallion symbol matches Terminal plinth. Journal says empire built the road. Wards are old imperial work. The Fort may exist because of the caves, not in spite of them. |

> **Sidebar:** The Premise crystallizes. The cave is a threat and a resource. The eggs are money and danger. The queen is an enemy and a mystery. Every choice about what to bring back, sell, destroy, or keep tells you and the table what kind of community The Fort is becoming. The Captain asks what they found. The Minister asks what they saw. Harrow asks what they're selling. The game begins.
