# Module 2

## *The Drowned Tower*

*A B/X teaching dungeon for 3–6 characters of levels 1–2, plus the Minister. GM-facing. Players never see this file.*

> **Homage note:** This module glosses the ruined-tower sample dungeon in the 1977 blue Basic rulebook: a broken tower over cellars, and cellars over a water cave that smugglers use. We take that shape as homage and as a teaching frame. All text, names, room contents and maps here are original to The Fort, and none of the source book's names, keys or read-aloud appear in it.

**Map:** [The Drowned Tower, DM copy](maps/drowned_tower.svg) (three panels: the Island, the Lamp Cellars, the Grotto).

## The Job

Captain Maren calls the party to the Hall the morning after they come back from the Gorgon Caves, or whenever this module starts.

> **Read aloud:** Maren has a lake chart spread on the council table, weighted at the corners with a whetstone and three cold cups. Her finger rests on the island nobody visits. "Fishermen see a light out there on calm nights. Low, by the water. And something big has started coming into the Shallows after dark. I want to know who lands on that island, and what moves in the water. Take a boat. Come back."

| Term | Detail |
|------|--------|
| **The ask** | Find out who lands on the island and what moves in the water. Report back. |
| **The pay** | 20 gp each from the common chest, and first pick of anything that isn't The Fort's. |
| **The boat** | One of the two rowboats (holds four with gear). The sailboat if they ask and Maren trusts them. |
| **What she doesn't say** | Since the Gorgon Queen fell, someone has been buying every cockatrice egg Harrow can find. Maren thinks that someone uses the island. She wants proof before she accuses a man she needs. |

### The Gorgon Queen, and Why the Eggs Matter

In play, in the Gorgon Caves, the Fae player domesticated a cockatrice chick, and **the chick's cry paralysed the Gorgon Queen**.

What happened next is **assumed** (the Year Two DM Guide marks it so; replace it freely): the Gorgon Queen is dead, and the wards in her chamber went dark when she died. Petra is flesh again and sleeps a lot. The iron cage in the Beast Pen is empty. If your table's Year One went another way, change the lines in this module that lean on it: the eye-wards (the table below, C6 and G8) and the Fighter's hook.

The chick is **Biddy**. She lives in the Beast Pen at The Fort and guards the henhouse. **Wick**, the twelve-year-old lamplighter, keeps her. Everyone keeps clear of her beak, and everyone on the lake has heard of her.

Word spreads. A cockatrice stopped a gorgon, and The Fort owns the one that did. Harrow paid 50 gp an egg last season. Now he pays 100, and his buyers pay him more. The buyers are **the Saltmen**. They keep their stock in the grotto under the island, and they pass the eggs on to people the ledger calls **"the Readers"**.

### The Throughline in One Breath

| Module 1 left | This module shows | Module 3 picks up |
|---------------|-------------------|-------------------|
| Eggs go to Harrow, and Harrow won't say where they go next | Harrow sells to the Saltmen. Their ledger sends the eggs up the Old Road to the Readers on the Ridge. | The Readers of the Unwritten, their temple on the Ridge, and why they want stone guardians |
| The Gorgon Queen's chamber had carved eyes: wards. They went dark when she died (assumed). | The same eyes ring the Door Beneath the Lake. Here they are shut, waiting. | The Readers want that door open. |
| The Elf Minister's warning: *"must not open the door beneath the lake"* | This is the door. It does not open in this module. | The final choice at the dark Terminal can tell a PC where it leads. |

### The Clock

The job has a deadline the party doesn't know about.

| Night after the party first lands | What happens |
|-----------------------------------|--------------|
| 1–3 | The Saltmen go about their work (see each room). The boat stays in the cave mouth by day. |
| 4 | **The last run.** At moonset the Saltmen load the eggs, the ledger, Vosk's strongbox and Vosk herself into the boat and row east. Two men stay behind to keep the cache. |
| Any night, if alarmed | Vosk leaves the next night instead. "Alarmed" means a Saltman escaped a fight, or the C12 post was found empty or dead. |

If the party is slow, the eggs and the ledger are gone, and the trail to the Readers goes cold until Module 3 picks it up on the Old Road. That is a fair result. Say so when it happens.

## What the DM Needs to Know

### Lamp Station Nine

The empire built a tower on the isle to watch one thing: the Door Beneath the Lake. The station was a lighthouse, an observatory and a guard post at once. Its lamp was a beacon, a lens for reading the water, and the key that commands the Warden. Its carved eyes are wards and sensors at the same time: they see heat and colour, and they hold things in place. Players can treat any of this as magic or as machinery. Both readings work.

Locals call it **the Drowned Tower** because its lowest rooms are below the waterline. The upper floors fell in a storm long after the last keeper died.

### Ostrel Vane, the Lampwright

| | |
|---|---|
| **Was** | Imperial magister. Last keeper of Station Nine. Grinder of lenses, keeper of the lamp, master of the Warden. |
| **Did** | Stayed when the supply boats stopped. Kept the Warden fed and the Door watched for forty years. When his keepers died, he riveted their oath-plates into the ward current so the dead would keep their rounds. |
| **Is now** | Bones in a chair in C7, with his notes in front of him. His notes change the climax. |
| **Wanted** | Someone to come after him. His last page asks whoever reads it to say the words for the dead. |

### The Saltmen

| | |
|---|---|
| **Are** | Ten smugglers on the isle and their boss. A courier, **Dace**, is away on a run up the Old Road. Coast folk in oilskins crusted white with salt. They run salt, salt fish, cloth and anything else past the coast's toll-takers. |
| **Use the isle because** | Nobody goes there. They cart goods from the coast to the lake's east shore and row the rest. The grotto is dry, hidden and guarded by a fish they have learned to feed. |
| **Sign** | An eye with a line through it, in chalk. It means "keep out". It also mocks the imperial eyes. |
| **Want** | Money, quiet, and to be gone before the Readers stop being polite. |
| **Will do** | Trade, bluff, bribe, threaten. Fight if cornered or if the cache is at stake. |
| **Won't do** | Swim in the pool. Open the study door. Go near the Door. |
| **Treat PCs as** | A problem to be priced. Most of them would rather be paid than fight. |

### Ketter Vosk

| | |
|---|---|
| **Is** | The Saltmen's boss. Lean, fifty, a salt-white braid, a voice that never rises. Reads and writes. Keeps the ledger herself. |
| **Wants** | Out. Alive, with the boat and the strongbox. |
| **Fears** | The Readers. "Polite people who don't blink." |
| **Knows** | Harrow sells her eggs. Dace carries them up the Old Road and hasn't come back from the last run. The Readers pay in faceless gold. They want the eggs, and they want "the way past the fish" to the Door. She lost two men learning that the fish can't be swum past. |
| **Will deal** | The ledger and the Readers' letter, for her life and her boat. She'll open the bidding at less. |

### The Warden

A giant catfish, older than The Fort, wearing a **bronze control collar** riveted shut by the empire. The collar binds it to guard the Door and to stay within the ring of lamp-stones. It circles the isle at dawn and dusk and lies by the Door the rest of the time.

It is not evil. It is a servant who has outlived its masters. *Detect Evil* finds nothing. The Paladin's and the Druid's players should be told that plainly.

How it behaves is in G7. How the party can change that is in C7. That link is the point of the module.

### The Door Beneath the Lake

A round door of black stone banded in bronze, fifteen feet across, set in the grotto's north wall under the cliff, half under water. It is reached by the pool's north arm: a ledge, or a swim. Carved eyes ring it, all shut. It faces north and opens toward the Deep (hex D1).

The Isle of Old Stone is the roof of **Halcyon**, Year Two's drowned lamp-city, and the Door Beneath the Lake is one face of the Isle way into it. Lamp Station Nine was Halcyon's lighthouse and watch-post. The Year Two DM Guide keys Halcyon; this module does not. If a player asks where the Door goes, the answer is "north, and down".

**Nobody opens the Door in this module.** No key here fits it. The lamp commands the Warden, not the Door. It is a hook for later, not a room to clear.

## The Crossing

Twelve miles of lake, north from The Fort's docks across the Shallows.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Time** | Four to five hours by rowboat. About three under sail with a fair wind. |
| **Leave at dawn** | Land at midday. A full afternoon on the isle, then camp or row home in the dark. |
| **Leave at midday** | Land near dusk. The Warden circles the isle at dusk. The party sees it from the boat. |
| **Row home** | Same time back. A party that leaves the isle at dusk rows the Shallows at night. |

> **Design Note:** The crossing is the first lesson about time. It costs half a day each way. Say so at the dock. Players who plan the trip around daylight are already playing the module.

### The Shallows (D3)

Fish by day. Singing at night.

By day the water is clear to the weed and full of perch. By night it sings: a low chord under the hull, felt in the oar-handles more than heard. Anyone who trails a hand in the water hears words in no language. The Gnome Druid hears the same hum as the lamp-stones. Nobody at The Fort has an explanation, and this module does not offer one.

### The Quiet of the Reed Shore (E3)

Stilt huts in the reeds on the lake's east side, an hour's detour. The people there are called **the Quiet**. They do not speak aloud. They answer with hands and with knotted cords.

| If the party | The Quiet |
|--------------|-----------|
| Asks about the isle | Draw a circle in the mud. Put eight stones round it. Put one large fish inside. Wipe it all out except the fish. |
| Asks about the boat with no lights | Tie a cord with four knots and a gap. (Four nights. The last run.) |
| Asks for a guide | One of them will come for a gift of salt. A guide points the safe landing (S1), refuses to set foot past the jetty, and waits in the boat. |
| Offers salt | They take it with both hands. Salt is worth more to them than coin. |
| Brings an egg | They back away into the reeds and do not come out. |

> **Design Note:** Keep the Quiet brief and strange. They are witnesses, not quest-givers. One scene, then the party rows on.

## The Dungeon Clock

*For a first-time DM. Photocopy this page. Keep it beside your dice.*

### Rule One: Plan, then Execute

Every room, every turn, runs in two phases.

| Phase | What happens |
|-------|--------------|
| **Plan** | Players talk, look and ask. You answer honestly. What is in plain view, they see. No dice. No time passes. |
| **Execute** | The caller says **"Execute."** Plans lock. Each player states one action. You resolve them. Now time passes. |

If a player acts during Plan ("I open the door"), ask once: **"Is that your plan, or your action?"** Looking is free. Doing costs time.

### The Turn

A **turn** is ten minutes. One Execute in the dungeon is one turn. In a turn the party can do one thing: walk and map 120 feet, search one 10-foot square each, listen at a door and open it, rest, or read a page.

**Each turn, in order:**

1. **Plan.** Let them talk.
2. **Execute.** One action each.
3. **Tick** one box on the track below.
4. **W box?** Roll a d6. On a 1, a wandering monster comes. Roll on this level's table.
5. **R box?** The party rests this turn, or takes –1 to hit and damage until it does.
6. **Light.** A torch lit at the start of a row goes out at its end.

### The Numbers

| Thing | Rule |
|-------|------|
| Turn | 10 minutes. A whole fight counts as one turn. |
| Round | 10 seconds, in a fight. |
| Exploring | 120 feet a turn at the slowest PC's pace, mapping as you go. Ground already mapped: three times as fast. |
| Torch | Burns 6 turns. Light 30 feet. One hand. |
| Lantern | One flask of oil burns 24 turns. Light 30 feet. Can be hooded. |
| Infravision | Elf, Elf Minister, Gnome 60 feet; half-elf 30 feet. Shapes and heat, no colour, no reading. Useless inside torchlight. |
| Wandering check | Every 2 turns, 1-in-6. Also roll at once after loud noise: a failed forcing, a fight, a shout. |
| Rest | One turn in six, or –1 to hit and damage until rested. |
| Search | One turn per 10-foot square, per searcher. Secret doors: 1-in-6 (elves 2-in-6). An elf passing within 10 feet gets a free 1-in-6. |
| Listen | Once per PC per door. 1-in-6 (demi-humans 2-in-6). The dead make no sound. |
| Stuck door | Force on 1–2 on a d6 (STR bonus adds). Each try is loud: roll a wandering check, and anything behind it is ready. |
| Locked door | Needs a key, a spell, or a Fate ability. Breaking it down takes 3 turns, each one loud. |
| Doors | Most doors are stuck. They swing shut unless spiked or wedged. Monsters open them freely. |
| Surprise | d6 each side, 1–2 surprised. A party with lights and noise rarely surprises anything. |

### Fate, in the Dungeon

| Rule | Detail |
|------|--------|
| **Fate Points** | 3 each. Refill after a full night's rest. |
| **Spend 1** | Reroll one attack, damage roll or save. Or +4 to a save after seeing the roll. |

## The Turn Is a Procedure

*A sidebar for tables with programmers or students in them, and for the DM who wants the whole loop in one look. Logo is the language children used to steer a turtle; this is the same idea with a party instead.*

```logo
TO TURN
  PLAN                                  ; talk, look, ask: no dice, no time
  EXECUTE                               ; the caller says it; one action each
  MAKE "CLOCK :CLOCK + 1                ; tick a box: ten minutes pass
  BURN 1                                ; every lit torch and lantern loses a turn
  IF 0 = REMAINDER :CLOCK 2 [WANDER]    ; d6, and on a 1 something comes
  IF 0 = REMAINDER :CLOCK 6 [REST]      ; rest now, or -1 to hit and damage
  IF FIGHTING? [ROUNDS]                 ; a fight runs in rounds, then counts as this turn
  IF BELOW? [TURN]                      ; do it again until the party climbs out
END
```

Students like to ask what `WANDER`, `REST` and `ROUNDS` are. Good. Each is its own procedure, and the rest of this module is them.

## Turtle Mapping

*The mapping lesson. Players learn dungeon space the way children learned geometry in Logo: by being the turtle. "Forward 50, right 90", not x and y.*

### The DM's Call Pattern

Describe every move **relative to the party's facing**. Say compass directions once, at the top of the stair. After that, use the turtle's words.

| Call | Example |
|------|---------|
| **Facing** | "You're facing the way the stair goes down." (Say it once. The mapper puts an arrow on the page.) |
| **Ahead** | "The passage runs 30 feet ahead." |
| **Turn** | "Then it bends right." / "It ends at a door." |
| **Sides** | "A door on your left, 10 feet along." / "An open arch on your right." |
| **Rooms** | "You step in. The room runs 20 feet ahead and 30 feet across. You came in at its right-hand corner." |
| **Exits** | Count them every time, left to right: "Three ways out: an arch on the left wall, a door straight ahead, the door behind you." |

Never say "north-east corner" after the first call. Say "the far right corner". The mapper turns the page. The players turn their bodies. That is the lesson.

### Worked Example: C1 to C4

The party comes down the trapdoor in S2. The stair goes down toward the north. Facing: the way the stair goes.

> **DM:** "The stair runs 30 feet ahead and drops about 20. At the bottom there's a 10-foot landing and an oak door straight ahead. The walls are close: 10 feet apart."
> **Mapper draws:** FORWARD 3 squares (stair). FORWARD 1 (landing). A door ahead.

They force the door into C2.

> **DM:** "You step in. You're at the right-hand end of a room: it runs 20 feet to your left and 20 feet ahead, and nothing to your right. Broken jars on racks. Exits: the whole left wall opens on steps going down; a door straight ahead in the far wall; the door behind you."
> **Mapper draws:** a box 3 squares wide by 2 deep, entry at its right corner. Left side open. A door ahead.

After the rats, they take the far door.

> **DM:** "The door opens on a passage. It runs 30 feet ahead and comes out in the side of a long hall, 20 feet across. The hall runs 60 feet to your left and 30 feet to your right. In the far wall, straight across from you: another door. Fifty feet to your left in that same wall: an open arch, dark. The left end has a short passage in it. The right end has another."
> **Mapper draws:** FORWARD 3. Then a long bar, 6 squares left and 3 right, 2 deep. Door across. Arch 5 squares left.

### Let the Map Be Wrong

Players will draw the hall too short, or put the arch in the wrong place. **Don't correct them.** The west loop (C4, C5, C10, C8 and back to C4) is where a wrong map shows itself. They walk round, come back into the hall through its north door, and the lines on their page don't meet. Now they know. They walk it again, counting, and fix it themselves.

The secret door in C1 does the same thing. When they find Vane's passage from the study side and come out at the bottom of the stair they started on, the mapper says "wait, *that's* where we are?" That moment teaches mapping better than anything you can explain.

> **Design Note:** The players' map is the thing they make and hold. It is the module's most important piece of treasure. Let them keep it between sessions. Their second trip in should run off their own map, not your memory.

## Retreat, Rest and Return

Most parties go in, run low, row home and come back. That is the shape of this module, not a failure.

| Running low on | Sign | What to do |
|----------------|------|------------|
| Light | Last torch lit | Say how many turns are left, plainly. Point out the oil in C2 and S3. |
| Hit points | Anyone under half | Remind them they can leave. A full rest at camp or at The Fort heals 1d3 HP a day (more with clerics). |
| Spells | Every caster spent | Spells come back after a night's sleep and study. The Minister's need a Terminal: The Fort's, or the dead screen in C11 if someone wakes it. |
| Fate | Under 2 points | Fate refills after a full rest. |

**Camping on the isle** is allowed. The cottage (S3) has walls. Roll one wandering check on the Surface table for the night. The Saltmen come up to the cottage on nights 1–3 (see S3).

### The Dungeon Notices

When the party leaves and comes back, apply every **triggered** change, then roll a d6 once on the **random** table.

**Triggered changes**

| If the party | Then, when they return |
|--------------|------------------------|
| Killed the C2 rats | 1d4 new giant rats in C2, from the drains. |
| Destroyed undead | They stay destroyed. Undead still standing resume their rounds: the C5 skeletons walk C5–C4–C2 and back once a turn. |
| Fought Saltmen and any escaped | C12 now holds five Saltmen, crossbows loaded. The Clock moves: Vosk leaves the next night. |
| Turned the C10 sluice | The stair (C3) refills a day later unless the wheel is wedged open. |
| Left the C7 east door unbarred | The Saltmen have been in the study. Roll on the random table twice and take result 5 as one of them. |
| Took the Warden's Lamp home | The ward ring in C6 is dead. The C11 zombies have walked to C6 and stand where the lamp was. |
| Freed the Warden | No Warden. The Door stands unguarded. The lamp-stones have gone dark. |

**Random (d6)**

| d6 | The dungeon has changed |
|----|-------------------------|
| 1 | Rats again: 1d4+1 giant rats in C4, eating something. |
| 2 | Fresh chalk. New Saltmen signs on every door from C1 on, and a tripwire bell across the C7–C12 passage. |
| 3 | Two stirges have moved into the dead end (C9). |
| 4 | High water. C3 has risen six steps; G6 is waist-deep. |
| 5 | Someone has read Vane's notes. One finding of the DM's choice is missing. It is now on Vosk's chart table in G4. |
| 6 | Quiet. Nothing has changed. Tell the players "nothing has changed": quiet is a result too. |

> **Design Note:** The restock table teaches that a dungeon is a place with its own life, not a level that waits. Read the change aloud when they come back down the stair. "Someone has been here" is the most frightening sentence in a dungeon.

# Part Two: The Island

The isle is half a mile across: rock, pine and old stone. Map panel 1 is at 100 feet to the square; the inset shows the tower and cottage at 10 feet.

## S1. The Broken Jetty

| | |
|---|---|
| **Size** | Jetty 10' wide, 60' long; its last 20' has fallen in |
| **Light** | Daylight, or none |
| **Exits** | North: path uphill through the pines to S2 (about 1,300'). East: shore path to S4. South: the lake. |
| **Encounter** | The Warden passes. No fight unless someone is in the water. |

> **Read aloud:** The jetty is old dressed stone, black with weed to the tideless waterline, and its far end has slumped into the lake like a tired arm. A post of grey oak stands where it meets the shore. Its iron ring is bright where a rope has lately worn it. Past the broken end the water lifts in one long, smooth swell, as if something the size of your boat turned over in its sleep; then the lake is flat again.

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| Mooring post | The rope burn is fresh: days, not weeks. Tar fibres in the ring. The Saltmen tar their ropes. Someone else lands here when the north side is rough. |
| The swell | The Warden on its dusk or dawn circuit. It is gone in a round. If the party watches the water for a full turn, they see it come round the east point and go west: it circles the isle, clockwise. |
| The fallen end | Walk past the sound stones and roll 1-in-6: the stones shift and the walker goes in. Not hurt, but wet, and in the water. In metal armour, haul them out next round. |
| Bootprints | On the path north: heavy boots, several men, a week old under fresh needles. They lead toward the tower. |
| A lamp-stone | Ten yards along the shore, a man-high stone with a carved eye and a bronze dish on top. One of eight. See S4. |

> **Art reference:** Otus ink, black on cream. A low frame: the broken jetty running out of the bottom edge, a rowboat tied to the post, and beyond the jetty's end a single long curve of water with nothing visible beneath it.

> **What This Room Teaches:** **Plan, then Execute.** This is where the gate is first taught. While they stand on the jetty, they may look and ask for free. When they act, they say so and a turn passes. It is also where they set **marching order and light**: the jetty is 10 feet wide, so two abreast. Have them draw the order on a card now, and say who holds the torch when they go below.

> **Design Note:** Run S1 slowly. Ask "What do you do?", let them talk, answer their looking for free, then say "Ready? Call Execute." The second time, they'll call it themselves. Obvious: tie up, walk up the path. Clever: watch the water for a turn and learn which way the Warden circles; that pays off in G7.

## S2. The Tower Shell

| | |
|---|---|
| **Size** | Round, 30' across inside. Walls 4' thick. |
| **Light** | Daylight from the open top |
| **Exits** | South: door gap and path to S1. East: 20' to S3. North: path to S5 (about 200'). Down: trapdoor to C1, once found. |
| **Encounter** | Crows (harmless, loud) |

> **Read aloud:** The tower is a drum of grey stone with the sky for a lid. Its upper floors lie on the ground floor in a hill of masonry, beams and green glass. A stair, each step a stone tongue set in the wall, climbs round the inside to a broken lip forty feet up, where crows sit and comment on you. Over the door, letters cut deep and filled with old verdigris.

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| Rubble | Fills the floor. Searching it takes **one turn** and finds the trapdoor. One searcher is enough. |
| Trapdoor | Iron-bound oak, ring handle, 4' square, near the middle. Lifting it lets out cold air that smells of lamp oil and wet stone. |
| The stair | One turn to climb up and come down. The crows mob whoever climbs: noise, so roll a wandering check at once. |
| Stair top | A view of the whole isle: jetty, cottage, the lamp-stones on the east point, and at the north cliff a **boat-shaped shadow** in the water, under the overhang. |
| Crows' nest | On the top ledge. Searching it takes one turn: a silver ring stamped with a lamp and the numeral IX (25 gp). |
| Green glass | Shards of the great lens, thick as a thumb. The station's light was green. |
| Lintel | Imperial Common: *STATION NINE. KEEP THE LIGHT. KEEP THE DOOR.* The Elf Minister and the Paladin read it at once. Others with INT 13+ puzzle it out in a turn. |

> **Art reference:** Otus ink, black on cream. Looking up from inside the drum: the spiral stair, crows black against a pale ring of sky, and the rubble below drawn in dense hatching with one straight edge showing through it.

> **What This Room Teaches:** **Searching costs time.** Finding the trapdoor is one turn. Climbing for the view is another. The nest is a third. Ask which they want. They can have all three, but the clock moves.

> **Design Note:** Obvious: climb for the view. Clever: search the rubble first. Both are right; the order they choose is the lesson. If nobody searches, the crows' noise brings a wandering check. If it's the Saltman pair, they glance at one corner of the rubble as they pass and spit. That shows the party where to look.

## S3. The Keeper's Cottage

| | |
|---|---|
| **Size** | 30' × 20'. Roof gone. |
| **Light** | Daylight |
| **Exits** | West: door facing the tower, S2 |
| **Encounter** | None by day. Nights 1–3: two Saltmen come up from S5 to sit by the fire, 2 hours after dark. |

> **Read aloud:** A keeper's cottage, stone to the shoulder and open to the weather above. Someone has slept here lately: the hearth holds a grey drift of ash with no rain in it, and fish bones picked clean. On the door, in fresh chalk, an eye has been drawn and struck through with one hard line.

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| Hearth | Ash less than a week old. A broken clay pipe of coast make. Searching the hearth square (one turn) finds a loose hearthstone and under it an iron key: it opens the locked door of C7. |
| Oil tin | On the shelf: a tin of lamp oil, two flasks' worth. |
| Chalk mark | An eye with a line through it. A Saltmen sign: "keep out". It appears again in the cellars. |
| Salt dish | A stone dish of coarse salt on the shelf. It strips leeches in G6 without harm. |
| Bed frame | Rotted rope. Under it, nothing. Searching it costs a turn and finds nothing; that is allowed. |

> **What This Room Teaches:** **Searching costs time, and you choose where.** Six squares, six turns to search it all. The key is under one of them. The oil and salt are in plain view, free. The bed is a turn spent for nothing.

> **Design Note:** Obvious: take the oil. Clever: search the hearth, because that's the only thing in the room someone has used. Teach players to search where the story points, not everywhere.

## S4. The Lamp-Stones

| | |
|---|---|
| **Size** | Three stones on the east point, 8' tall, 20' apart. Five more stand alone round the shore. |
| **Light** | Daylight |
| **Exits** | West: shore path to S1. North-west: path to S5. |
| **Encounter** | None |

> **Read aloud:** Three standing stones lean together on the point like old men at a rail. Each bears one carved eye at the height of your own, and each wears a bronze dish on its head, crusted green and full of rain. Up close they hum, low, as a hive hums in winter. The hum is in your teeth before it is in your ears.

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| The hum | Louder at dawn and dusk. The Paladin feels it as the wards' pulse. The Druid hears the same note as the Shallows. |
| The eyes | The same carving as the Gorgon Queen's chamber. Anyone who was there knows it on sight. |
| Bronze dishes | Lamp-dishes. Pour in a flask of oil and light it: the stone's eye glows green for 6 turns, and the Warden comes to that stone and circles there. It leaves the pool empty while it does. |
| The fallen stone | One of the five lone stones lies broken on the south shore. Bronze wire runs out of the break into the rock: the stones are posts in a fence, and the fence is wired. |
| The ring | Eight stones in all, round the shore. The Warden cannot pass outside them. |

> **Art reference:** Otus ink, black on cream. Three stones in silhouette against open water, each eye drawn as a hard almond, the bronze dishes hatched dark, and fine radiating lines in the air between the stones to show the hum.

> **What This Room Teaches:** **The isle is one machine.** The stones on the shore, the eyes in the cellars and the collar on the fish are parts of one thing. Reading the site starts on the surface.

> **Design Note:** Obvious: look, touch, move on. Clever: light a dish and watch what comes. A party that does this early learns that the Warden answers to the station's light. That is half the climax, found for the price of one flask of oil.

## S5. The North Cliff

| | |
|---|---|
| **Size** | A 50' cliff over the lake, with a cleft in its top |
| **Light** | Daylight |
| **Exits** | South: path to S2. South-east: path to S4. Down: knotted rope 50' to the ledge in G1. |
| **Encounter** | By day, one Saltman sits in the boat below, fishing. He looks up 1-in-6 each turn the party stands at the edge. |

> **Read aloud:** The pines stop and the isle ends in a drop of grey rock to dark water. Below, the cliff foot is hollowed into a cave mouth, and in its throat a long open boat rides at a line, a man in an oilskin sitting in the stern with a rod. A cleft splits the cliff top beside you; a tarred rope, knotted every foot, is tied off to a pine root and drops into it.

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| The boat | The boat-shaped shadow seen from S2. Thirty feet, oars and a lug sail. The Saltmen's only way off the isle. |
| The rope | Hidden in the cleft: search one turn, or follow the bootprints from S3. Climbing down with it takes one turn and needs no roll. In plate, two turns. |
| The cave mouth | G1. The channel inside runs into the dark. |
| Bootprints | Worn deep between the cleft and the cottage. |

> **What This Room Teaches:** **There is more than one way in.** The rope reaches the grotto without passing a single cellar. It is the fast road to the climax and the worst one: it lands the party at the Saltmen's front door, unprepared.

> **Design Note:** Obvious: climb down. Clever: lie still and watch for a turn or two. The fisherman rings a bell before the boat rows out; the party learns the bell matters before they ever see it.

# Part Three: The Lamp Cellars

Twenty feet under the tower. Dressed stone, low vaults, the smell of old oil. Map panel 2. Wandering checks start in C2.

## C1. The Cellar Stair

| | |
|---|---|
| **Size** | Stair 10' wide, 30' long, falling 20'. A 10' landing at the bottom. |
| **Light** | None |
| **Exits** | Up: trapdoor to S2. North: stuck oak door to C2. East: secret door to Vane's passage (50') and C7. |
| **Encounter** | None |

> **Read aloud:** The stair goes down into a cold that has been waiting a long time. Every ten feet an iron hook juts from the wall for a lamp that is no longer there. At the bottom, on a landing hardly bigger than a cupboard, an oak door stands shut, black with age and swollen in its frame.

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| The oak door | **Stuck.** Forcing: 1–2 on a d6, STR bonus adds. The Fighter's heavy hammer adds +1. Each try makes noise: roll a wandering check, and the rats in C2 are not surprised. |
| Listening | 1-in-6 (demi-humans 2-in-6). Success: scratching and squeaks beyond. |
| East wall of the landing | A **secret door**: a stone panel that pivots on an iron pin. Searching the landing (one turn) finds it 1-in-6, elves 2-in-6. From the far side it has a plain lever and is obvious. |
| Lamp hooks | Bronze, worth nothing, but each one is stamped IX. |

> **What This Room Teaches:** **Listening and doors.** Listen first, then force. Each try is a noise, and noise brings the clock.

> **Design Note:** Obvious: shoulder the door. Clever: listen, then oil the hinges from a flask (the next forcing roll succeeds on 1–3). The secret door is here so that it can be found late, from the study side, and close the map into a loop.

## C3. The Flooded Stair

| | |
|---|---|
| **Size** | 40' × 20'. A broad stair going down to the west. |
| **Light** | None |
| **Exits** | East: arch to C2. Down (west): flooded stair to G6. |
| **Encounter** | None |

> **Read aloud:** A broad stair keeps going down where the cellar should end, step after step into water as black and still as ink in a well. Your light lies on it and does not go in. As you watch, the water creeps up one step, quietly, as if it had been waiting for you to look away.

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| The water | From the twelfth step down, the stair is flooded all the way to G6, about 30' of it under water. |
| Restless lake | Each turn the party is here, roll a d6. On 1–2 the lake is restless: the water rises one step. When it calms, it falls back over a turn. |
| Swimming it | Two rounds holding breath, with a rope line tied off at the top. No rope: save vs. death or come up in the wrong place and lose a turn. Metal armour: walk the steps on the rope, three rounds. |
| The drain | Under the water, at the foot of the stair, an iron gate. The sluice wheel in C10 opens it and drains the stair in two turns, leaving a wet slope, knee-deep at the bottom. |

> **Art reference:** Otus ink, black on cream. A stair of crisp ruled steps descending left, the water drawn as flat parallel strokes that swallow the lower steps, one bright torch-flare reflected on its surface.

> **What This Room Teaches:** **The dungeon moves.** The water is a door with a timer, not a trap. It doesn't hurt you; it makes you choose: swim now, or find the way to drain it.

> **Design Note:** Obvious: swim. Clever: find the sluice (C10) and walk down dry-ish. The demo ends here, with the water rising. In the module the stair is a shortcut to the grotto that drops the party at the pool's edge, past the Saltmen and before the study. Let them take it if they insist. The Warden will teach the rest.

## G8. The Door Beneath the Lake

| | |
|---|---|
| **Size** | About 70' × 45', under the north cliff, west of the cave mouth. The north half is deep water; the Door's lower half is in it. |
| **Light** | None |
| **Exits** | South: the north arm, by its ledge or by swimming, to G7. |
| **Encounter** | None. The Warden, if it is still bound and not stood down. |

> **Read aloud:** The ledge ends in a low cave under the cliff, its far half flooded and still. | Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| The Door | Black stone, bronze bands, no handle, no lock, no seam you can find. Reaching it from the dry ledge means a 20' swim. It faces north, toward the Deep. It is one face of the Isle way into **Halcyon**, the drowned lamp-city under the lake, which the Year Two DM Guide keys; Lamp Station Nine was Halcyon's lighthouse and watch-post. Do not explain it to the players. If asked: "north, and down". |
| The eyes | Twelve, shut. The same make as the eyes in the Gorgon Queen's chamber, which went dark when she died (assumed), and as the open eyes of the ward room (C6). These are shut, waiting. |
| Listening | Ear to the stone: very far down, the singing of the Shallows. |
| *Detect Magic* | Every other made thing in the station glows. The Door does not. Whatever it is, it isn't this kind of thing. |
| *Detect Evil* | Nothing. The Paladin is not reassured. |
| The votive shelf | A stone shelf beside the Door: keepers' offerings. A gold disc stamped with an open eye, 400 gp. Taking it is the Paladin's call to object. |
| Vosk's tools | If the Saltmen tried: a crowbar, bent, and a chalk copy of the eyes on a board. The Readers asked for it. |

**If they try to open it:** it doesn't. Nothing in this module opens it. Each try takes a turn and brings one of these, in order: the eyes twitch under their lids; the water in the cave rises a foot; the lamp-stones on the surface hum loud enough to hear down here; that night every PC dreams of a long stair going down, north. No damage. No door.

> **Art reference:** Otus ink, black on cream. The Door square in frame, small figures on the ledge for scale, the shut eyes drawn with single heavy lids, and below the waterline the bronze bands continuing down in broken reflected strokes.

> **What This Room Teaches:** **Not every door is for opening.** Some are for knowing about. This one points at the rest of the campaign.

> **Design Note:** The Door is on the grotto's north side so that it opens toward the Deep, not toward the station. Obvious: try to open it. Clever: leave it shut, copy the eyes, and ask who else wants it open. The Elf Minister's warning lands here. Let her player have the first word.
