# The Lake Outpost & The Petrified Caves

## The World (What Everyone Knows)

There was an empire. It fell. Nobody agrees on when or why. The old roads are broken. The old cities are ruins. The old magic is in the walls and in the ground and sometimes it wakes up.

What's left are islands and coastlines and the settlements that cling to them. The seas between are dark and deep and things move in them. Trade ships come when they come. Soldiers don't come at all anymore. The frontier is on its own.

This is the frontier.

---

## The Lake Outpost

A wooden palisade around thirty buildings at the edge of a lake, backed by forest, two days' walk from the nearest fishing village. It wasn't supposed to be permanent. It is now.

### What's Here

**The Palisade.** Sharpened logs, 10' high, a single gate facing the lake path. One watchtower. It keeps animals out. It might slow down something worse.

**The Shrine.** A small stone building in the center of the outpost, open-sided under a weathered roof. Inside: the Sacred Terminal — a screen set into a stone plinth, still powered by something nobody understands. It displays the traditional hymns on loop, scrolling text in a language that's half-familiar, half-ancient. It used to answer questions. It used to display new information. Now it just cycles. The Minister tends it. People still come to watch the words scroll past. They bring offerings — bread, wildflowers, a coin — and set them at the base of the plinth.

**The Hall.** The largest building. Council meetings, meals when the weather's bad, a place to sleep if you don't have a roof. Captain Maren runs the outpost from here, loosely.

**The Forge.** The Fighter knows it. Anvil, bellows, a decent coal supply. It can make tools and mend armor. It can't make anything fancy.

**The Market.** Three stalls that operate when there's anything to sell. A traveling merchant comes through every two or three weeks with goods from the coast. Prices are high.

**The Beast Pen.** Behind the forge. A few goats, chickens. And one cage, heavy iron, that holds something the militia caught in the forest last month. The Fighter built the cage. The Captain hasn't decided what to do with what's inside.

**The Docks.** Two rowboats and a small sailboat at the lake's edge. The lake is calm most days. There's an island in the middle that nobody goes to.

### Who's Here

**Captain Maren.** Militia leader. Practical, tired, loyal to the outpost. She wants security. She'll make deals she doesn't like if it keeps people alive. She was a soldier in the empire's army before the collapse. She doesn't talk about it.

**Old Harrow.** The merchant. Comes from the fishing village. Sells tools, cloth, dried fish, lamp oil. Buys furs, herbs, salvage from ruins. He knows things about the region. He trades in information as much as goods.

**The Families.** Twelve families, roughly fifty people. Farmers, woodcutters, a fisherman, a tanner. They came here because there was nowhere else. They stay because leaving is worse. Some have children. The children play near the shrine.

**The Militia.** Six adults who take shifts on the wall. Volunteers, not soldiers. They have spears, leather armor, and one crossbow. Captain Maren drills them when she can.

## What's Out There

### The Forest (South and West)

Dense, old-growth. Game trails, streams, deadfalls. The Hunter knows it. The first half-mile is mapped and trapped — snare lines, trail markers. Beyond that, it gets thick and quiet. There are ruins in there. Old foundations. Something built roads through this forest once.

**Known Threats:** Wolves (seasonal), boar (territorial), giant spiders (deeper in, unconfirmed). The Hunter has been tracking something larger for weeks.

### The Lake (North)

Deep, cold, clear. Fish are plentiful near shore. The center is deep enough that you can't see bottom. There's an island — rocky, maybe half a mile across, with the remains of something built on it. Old stone. Nobody from the outpost has gone there.

**Known Threats:** Something in the lake comes close to shore at night. Ripples. No confirmed sighting. The Minister queried the Terminal about water purification; the harvest doubled, and then the thing got closer.

### The Coast Path (East)

A day's walk to the shore, two days to the fishing village. The path is clear but exposed. Bandits have been seen. Or scouts — the militia isn't sure. The Thief may know more.

**Known Threats:** At least one organized group of armed strangers operating along the coast. Bootprints, cold campfires, signs of observation. Their purpose is unknown.

### The Caves (Northwest, 3 hours from the outpost)

A limestone ridge above the forest canopy. Cave mouths are visible from the watchtower on clear days. Hunters avoid them. Animals avoid them.

Last month, a patrol of three militia went to investigate strange lights near the caves. Two came back. The third — **Petra** — didn't. The two who returned don't remember what happened clearly. They were confused, sluggish. The Minister examined them and said: "It's like they were somewhere else."

Three days ago, a woodcutter found Petra at the forest edge. She was standing upright, eyes open, and completely turned to stone.

## The Petrified Caves — Tutorial Dungeon

### The Hook

Captain Maren calls the PCs together. Petra was found petrified on the forest edge, posed as if walking home. Two more militia refuse to go near the ridge. Something in those caves did this. Maren needs someone to go in, find out what's doing it, and stop it — or at least learn enough to know if the outpost is in danger.

The Minister is coming because Petra was her friend. She doesn't say this. She says "you'll need healing."

### The Approach

Three hours through the forest. The Hunter leads. The trail is game-track until the last half-mile, where the trees thin and the undergrowth dies. The ground is rocky. Limestone. The air smells mineral — wet stone.

The cave mouth is wide, fifteen feet across, low ceiling. Cold air flows out. Inside, the passage descends at a gentle angle for a hundred feet before opening into Room 1.

**DM Note:** The approach is a good place for the Thief's "I Was Already Here" or the Hunter's "I've Seen the Signs." Let them establish a fact before they go underground.

---

### Room 1: The Garden of Stone

**Size:** 30' × 20'
**Light:** None (total darkness without torches)
**Features:** The floor is smooth limestone. Standing in the room are **six stone figures** — animals and people, scattered as if they were caught mid-step. A petrified deer. A fox. Two figures in armor from a style nobody recognizes. A woman holding a basket. A child.

None of them are Petra.

**What's here:**
- The figures are cold and solid. *Detect Magic* reveals a faint aura of transmutation on each.
- The armored figures carry stone weapons. They were soldiers. The Wizard's "I've Read About This" can establish who they were and when.
- The child's face is calm. Not afraid. Whatever happened was fast.

**Encounter:** None in this room. This room is for atmosphere and dread. Let the players examine. Let them talk. Let the Minister touch the child's stone face and say nothing.

**Trap:** A pressure plate near the center of the room (Find Traps, 1-in-6 for non-thieves, thief skill for Thief/Hunter). Triggers a grinding sound from deeper in the caves — doesn't hurt anyone, but alerts what's inside that visitors have arrived. If triggered, Room 3 is prepared for the party.

**Loot:** The woman's basket contains a stone apple. It's worth 25gp as a curiosity. Taking it feels wrong.

---

### Room 2: The Mirror Pool

**Size:** 40' × 30', natural cavern, uneven floor
**Light:** Faint phosphorescence from the pool (dim, 10' radius)
**Features:** A still pool of water fills the center of the room, roughly 15' across. The water is unnaturally reflective — mirror-flat, no ripples, even when touched. The ceiling is covered in stalactites. Two passages lead deeper: one wide (to Room 3), one narrow (to Room 4, a squeeze).

**What's here:**
- Looking into the pool shows your reflection — but delayed. Your reflection moves a half-second after you do. This is unsettling but not dangerous.
- If someone puts a hand in the water, it's cold. Very cold. And the reflection doesn't match — it shows your hand somewhere else. A different room. (This is a one-way window to Room 5. The DM can describe what the boss chamber looks like from above.)
- There are scratch marks on the walls near the narrow passage. Something clawed its way through — or was dragged.

**Encounter:** **2 × Cave Lizards** (HD 1, AC 7 [12], ATK 1 × bite 1d4+1, MV 60', ML 7)
They're drinking from the pool when the party arrives. They're not hostile unless cornered or the party gets between them and the exit. If the party is quiet (Move Silently or just cautious play), they can avoid this fight entirely.

**If the party fights:** The lizards are fast but fragile. They try to flee past the party toward Room 1. If one escapes, nothing happens — they're just animals. If the party kills both, the Minister says nothing, but she notices.

**Loot:** On the bottom of the pool (requires reaching into the cold water): a bronze medallion, tarnished. It bears a symbol the Minister recognizes — the same symbol on the Terminal's plinth. Worth nothing as coin. Worth everything as a question.

---

### Room 3: The Nesting Hall

**Size:** 50' × 30', high ceiling (20'+), natural columns
**Light:** None
**Features:** The ceiling is thick with what looks like mud-dauber nests — dozens of them, clustered around the stalactites. Some are broken open. Some are sealed. The floor is littered with bones — small animal bones, bird skeletons. And feathers. Metallic feathers. Bronze-colored.

**What's here:**
- The nests contain **cockatrice eggs** (stone-grey, warm to the touch). Most are empty. Three are intact. Each is worth 50gp to the right buyer. Each is also potentially dangerous (DM's discretion — do they hatch?).
- The metallic feathers are from the cockatrices. They're beautiful and faintly magical. *Detect Magic* shows transmutation. A feather can be used as a material component to cast *Stone to Flesh* (if the party ever finds that spell). This is the key to saving Petra.
- If the pressure plate in Room 1 was triggered, **2 × Cockatrices** are here, awake and aggressive. If it wasn't triggered, they're roosting on the ceiling and the party can try to sneak through (Move Silently, group check).

**Encounter:** **2 × Cockatrice** (HD 5**, AC 6 [13], ATK 1 × beak 1d6 + petrification, MV 90' / 180' flying, ML 7, Save vs. Petrification or turn to stone)

These are the real threat. One hit that gets past your save and you're a statue. The Fighter needs to be in front. The Wizard's *Sleep* doesn't work (too many HD). *Shield* or *Protection from Evil* might matter.

**Tactics:** Cockatrices are territorial, not smart. They dive from the ceiling, peck, and flap back up. They target whoever is closest to the nests. They flee if reduced to half HP.

**DM Note:** This is where someone might get petrified. That's okay. The feathers are in this room. The information to cure petrification is here. The problem and the solution are in the same place. If a PC gets petrified, the session becomes about rescuing them — which is a better story than a replacement character.

**Loot:** 3 × cockatrice eggs (50gp each, dangerous), 6 × bronze feathers (material component, valuable to wizards), bones contain 30gp in scattered coins from previous victims.

---

### Room 4: The Squeeze (Secret/Optional)

**Size:** 5' × 5' passage, then a 10' × 10' chamber
**Light:** None
**Access:** The narrow passage from Room 2. A human can fit through by removing armor and pack (1 turn to strip, 1 turn to squeeze through). The Fighter in chain mail has to leave it behind or not go.

**What's here:**
- A skeleton in robes, long dead, clutching a leather satchel. This was a wizard. A long time ago.
- The satchel contains: a scroll of *Stone to Flesh* (single use), a journal in a language the Wizard can read with *Read Magic*, and a silver dagger (1d4, +1 to hit, counts as magical).
- The journal describes this cave system. The wizard came here to study the cockatrices and the pool. The last entry says: *"The queen is below the pool. She does not move. She does not eat. She watches. I think she has been watching since the empire fell. I think the empire knew she was here. I think that's why they built the road."*

**DM Note:** This is a pure exploration reward. The scroll is the cure for petrification. The journal is lore. The Wizard's Fate ability can fire here — "I've read about this wizard. They were from the Academy of [name the player invents]. They specialized in transmutation."

---

### Room 5: The Queen's Chamber

**Size:** 40' × 40', domed ceiling, smooth carved walls
**Light:** The pool in Room 2 acts as a skylight — faint phosphorescence from above casts dim blue-green light
**Features:** The walls are carved. Not natural stone — shaped, deliberately, a long time ago. Spiraling patterns. Eyes. Dozens of carved eyes. In the center of the room: a stone throne. On the throne: **the Basilisk Queen.**

**What's here:**
- **Petra** is here. Standing ten feet from the throne, turned to stone, her hand outstretched as if she was trying to touch the basilisk. Or push it away.
- The carved eyes on the walls are wards. The Wizard's Fate ability can identify them. The Minister's "The Terminal Remembers" can connect them to the outpost's founding — the founder's log references this chamber.
- The throne is old. Older than the outpost. Older than the empire, maybe. Something put this here on purpose.

**Boss Encounter:** **The Basilisk Queen** (HD 6+1**, AC 4 [15], ATK 1 × bite 1d10 + petrification gaze, MV 60', ML 9, Save vs. Petrification or turn to stone — anyone meeting her gaze)

**Gaze Attack:** At the start of each round, every character *facing* the Basilisk must save vs. Petrification. Characters can choose to avert their eyes (-4 to hit) or fight blind (-6 to hit, need someone calling targets). **Mirrors** reflect the gaze — if someone holds a mirror and the Basilisk sees its own reflection, it must save or be petrified itself (this is the "trick" solution).

**The Fighter's Moment:** Shield Wall. Stand in front. Avert your eyes and hold the line. Everyone else fights from behind you or uses the trick.

**The Wizard's Moment:** If they have *Sleep*, it won't work (too many HD). If they have *Light*, they can cast it on the Basilisk's eyes — blinding it for 1d4+1 rounds (save negates), removing the gaze attack temporarily. This is the smart play.

**The Thief's Moment:** Backstab with averted eyes is hard (-4 to hit, so net +0 with backstab bonus). But if someone blinds the Basilisk first, the Thief can sneak behind it and backstab for double damage. Set up the combo.

**The Hunter's Moment:** Ranged ambush from the entrance. Bow attacks at -4 (averted eyes) but safe from melee. If the Basilisk is blinded, the Hunter fires freely.

**The Minister's Moment:** She walks toward Petra. She's going to try to touch the statue. She's going to close her eyes and reach for whatever answers her — the Terminal, the place, the old god, or nothing. Whether anything happens is up to the DM's choice about the Minister's secret, or the player's if the Minister is a PC.

**If the Basilisk is killed:** The gaze fades. The wards on the walls pulse once and go dark. Petra does *not* automatically un-petrify — that requires *Stone to Flesh* (from the scroll in Room 4) or the bronze feathers as a component for a future casting. The petrified figures in Room 1 remain stone. This is an old evil. Killing the queen doesn't undo what she's done.

**If the Basilisk is petrified by its own reflection:** Poetic. The queen becomes the newest statue in her own gallery. But is she dead? Or just... waiting? The wards on the walls stay lit. The Minister says: "She's still in there. I can feel it."

**Loot:**
- "Crown of the Stone Court" — a circlet on the throne. AC +1, and once per day the wearer can *speak* to a petrified creature for one minute. They're aware in there. They can answer.
- 200gp in old imperial coins scattered around the base of the throne.
- The throne itself is immovable.

## After the Caves

The party returns to the outpost with information, treasure, and a problem.

**What they learned:** Something old lives under the ridge. The empire knew about it. The outpost may have been built because of it, not in spite of it.

**What they have to decide:**
- Can Petra be saved? (Yes, with the scroll or the feathers — but the feathers require a spell nobody at the outpost knows yet.)
- What do they do with the eggs? (Sell them? Destroy them? Hatch them?)
- Is the queen dead or just contained?
- What's the connection between the caves and the shrine?

**The Premise hits:** The basilisk is a threat. But the eggs are worth money. The feathers are magical components. The crown lets you talk to the dead-but-not-dead. This cave is a *resource* as much as a danger. What is the outpost willing to do?

The Minister has an opinion. The Captain has an opinion. The PCs have choices. The game begins.
